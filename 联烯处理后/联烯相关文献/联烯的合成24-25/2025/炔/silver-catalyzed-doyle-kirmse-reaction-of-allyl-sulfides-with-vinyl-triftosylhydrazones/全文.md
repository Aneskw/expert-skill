# Silver-Catalyzed Doyle−Kirmse Reaction of Allyl Sulfides with Vinyl Triftosylhydrazones

Ziying Li, Zhaohong Liu,\* Paramasivam Sivaguru, Yong Yang, Karunanidhi Murali, Kuan Wang, and Xihe Bi\*

ABSTRACT: Here, we report a general and practical Doyle− Kirmse reaction of allyl/propargyl sulfides with donor-only vinyl carbenes generated in situ from vinyl triftosylhydrazones in the presence of a silver catalyst. This protocol features mild conditions, exhibits a broad substrate scope and exceptional functional group tolerance, and provides corresponding 1,5-dienyl and 1,4-enallenyl sulfides in high yields. [2,3]-Sigmatropic rearrangements of onium ylides are an important and fundamental transformation in organic chemistry for synthesizing valuable heteroatom-containing compounds with high stereoselectivity.<sup>1</sup> Among these, the [2,3]- sigmatropic rearrangement of sulfonium ylides generated from carbenes and allyl/propargyl sulfides, known as the Doyle− Kirmse (D−K) reaction,<sup>2</sup> ofers a potentially general method for the stereoselective construction of a high-value sulfurcontaining quaternary or tertiary carbon center, which has found applications in the synthesis of bioactive molecules and natural products. Over the decades, various transition metal and metal-free D−K reactions using tetramethylsilane (TMS)- stabilized<sup>3</sup> or electron-withdrawing group (EWG)-derived diazo reagents<sup>4−7</sup> as well as a few donor-only diazomethane reagents<sup>8</sup> and N-sulfonyl-1,2,3-triazoles<sup>9</sup> as carbene precursors have been established. In contrast, the utilization of α-vinyl diazo compounds in D−K reactions remains challenging and scarcely explored because of their propensity to undergo intramolecular cyclization to form pyrazoles.<sup>10</sup> Despite these challenges, Wang and Feng’s groups successfully employed vinyl diazoacetates<sup>4e</sup> or vinyldiazo compounds with chiral Oppolzer’s camphor sultam<sup>6a</sup> or pyrazoleamide<sup>6b</sup> auxiliary as vinylcarbene precursors for the enantioselective D−K reaction (Figure 1A). However, to the best of our knowledge, there has been no general catalytic system for the D−K reaction with donor-substituted α-vinyldiazomethanes as carbene precursors to date due to their inherent reactivity. A single example of a rhodium-catalyzed D−K reaction of phenyl allyl sulfide with in situ generated vinyldiazomethane from tosylhydrazone was found in a report by Wang and co-workers, although providing the desired product in 48% yield.<sup>8a</sup> In 2015, Wang and coworkers used cyclopropenes as vinylcarbene precursors in the D−K reaction to bypass the general reactivity issues of α- vinyldiazomethanes as donor diazo compounds (Figure 1A).<sup>11</sup>

Recently, we discovered vinyl triftosylhydrazones as easily decomposable diazo surrogates and addressed the general issue of the reactivity of α-vinyldiazomethane toward pyrazole formation by merging it with a bulky silver catalyst to generate highly electrophilic silver vinylcarbenes.<sup>12</sup> Encouraged by our success in transition-metal-catalyzed carbene transfer reactions using vinyl triftosylhydrazones as vinylcarbene precursors,<sup>13,14</sup> we envision that the merger of vinyl triftosylhydrazones with silver catalysis might govern the highly eficient D−K reaction on α-vinyldiazomethanes. Herein, we report a general and eficient silver-catalyzed D−K reaction between allyl/propargyl sulfides and α-vinyldiazomethanes derived from vinyl triftosylhydrazones (Figure 1B). This approach provides practical access to diverse 1,5-dienyl or 1,4-enallenyl sulfides with high eficiency. Dienyl sulfides are valuable core structures in biologically active molecules and versatile building blocks for the synthesis of value-added products.<sup>15</sup>

To test our hypothesis, we employed the reaction of α- cinnamyl N-sulfonyl hydrazone 1 and allyl phenyl sulfide $^ { 2 , }$ using NaH as a base, as a model system to identify a suitable catalyst (Table 1; for details, see Tables S1−S5 of the Supporting Information). Initially, the reaction using bulky $\mathrm { T p } ^ { \mathrm { \hat { B r } _ { 3 } } } \mathrm { A g } ( \mathrm { t h f } )$ as a catalyst in chloroform (CHCl ) at $ 6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \dot { \mathrm { { C } } }$ provided the desired product 3 in 82% nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) yield (entry 1 in Table 1). Delightfully, the relatively more electrophilic silver catalyst $\mathrm { T p } ^ { \mathrm { ( C F _ { 3 } ) _ { 2 } } } \mathrm { A g ( t h f ) }$ produced the target product 3 in superior yield (97% NMR yield, entry 2). In both cases, only a negligible amount of pyrazole ${ \mathfrak { z } } ^ { \flat }$ was detected. Other catalysts commonly used for $\mathrm { D - K }$ reactions, such as AgOTf, $\mathrm { C u } ( \mathrm { C H } _ { 3 } \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 4 } \mathrm { P F } _ { 6 } ,$ and $\mathrm { R h } _ { 2 } ( \mathrm { O A c } ) _ { 4 } , ^ { 4 \mathrm { c } , \mathrm { e } , 6 \mathrm { a } , 8 }$ aforded the desired product 3, albeit in a decreased yield along with more undesired product 3′ (entries $_ { 3 - 5 ) }$ . Other solvents, such as dichloromethane $\left( \mathrm { C H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C l } _ { 2 } \right)$ and trifluorotoluene $\left( \mathrm { P h C F } _ { 3 } \right)$ , did not improve the yield of product 3, but 1,4-dioxane yielded pyrazole (3′) as a major product (entries $_ { 6 - 8 ) }$ . Increasing or decreasing the reaction temperature could negatively afect the reaction eficiency (entries 9 and 10). The reactions involving N-nosylhydrazone $\mathbf { 1 6 } ^ { 1 6 }$ and N-tosylhydrazone $\mathbf { 1 } \mathbf { c } ^ { 1 7 }$ also proceeded well but aforded the desired product 3 in lower yields (entries 11 and 12), suggesting the superior performance of triftosylhydrazone compared to other N-sulfonyl hydrazones.<sup>18</sup> Interestingly, when an excessive amount of triftosylhydrazone 1a was employed, product 3 preferentially underwent cyclopropylation instead of proceeding with the second D−K rearrangement (Table S5 of the Supporting Information).

With the optimized reaction conditions in hand, we explored the scope of vinyl triftosylhydrazones derived from various $\alpha { , } \beta { - }$ unsaturated aldehydes, using allyl phenyl sulfide 2 as the reaction partner (Scheme 1). A variety of α-(arylvinyl) triftosylhydrazones, bearing electron-withdrawing or electrondonating substituents at the para, meta, or ortho position of the aryl ring, were well-tolerated and provided the desired 1,5- dienyl sulfides 3−20 in high to excellent yields. Vinyl triftosylhydrazones containing medicinally relevant (hetero)- aryl rings, such as naphthalene, furan, and thiophene, underwent the reaction smoothly, afording the target products 21−23 in good yields. This silver-catalyzed system was also applicable to vinyl triftosylhydrazones bearing non-aryl substituents, including methyl (24 and 29), n-butyl (25), phenylethyl (26), cyclohexyl (27), and (phenylmethoxy)- methyl (28) groups. Likewise, a set of 3,3-disubstituted and 2,3-disubstituted vinyl triftosylhydrazones could also be efectively activated by the current conditions, leading to the formation of multisubstituted 1,5-dienyl sulfide products 30− 34 in 48−84% yields. 2,4-Pentadienal-derived triftosylhydrazones also serve as suitable substrates, furnishing corresponding 1,5,7-trienyl sulfide products 35 and 36 in 74 and 55%, respectively, which are common skeletons in bioactive natural products and drug molecules.<sup>19</sup> Finally, we examined the reactivity of vinyl triftosylhydrazones derived from $\alpha { , } \beta { - }$ unsaturated ketones, which underwent smooth reaction to deliver desired 1,5-dienyl sulfides containing a quaternary carbon center in good yields (37−39). These compounds represent the most potent inhibitors of stromelysin-1.<sup>20</sup>

Next, we surveyed the scope of allyl sulfides using vinyl triftosylhydrazone 1a as the constant coupling reagent (Scheme 2A). Aryl allyl sulfides substituted with various substituents, such as methyl, methoxy, isopropyl, halogens, and trifluoromethyl groups, reacted smoothly to aford corresponding products 40−51 in good to excellent yields. Replacing the phenyl ring of aryl allyl sulfides with electron-rich naphthalene (52) and thiophene (53) rings aforded the desired product in high yields, while 2-methyl furyl allyl sulfide (54) resulted in a poor yield. Similarly, benzyl-, alkyl-, alkyl ester-, and allylsubstituted allyl sulfides (55−59) were also compatible with the reaction conditions. In the case of diallyl sulfide, the desired product 59 was exclusively obtained in 80% yield under standard conditions. Intriguingly, upon adjustment of the stoichiometric ratio of compound 1a to diallyl sulfide from 1:2 to 2:1, no detectable double D−K reaction product was observed. Instead, product 59 was isolated in 63% yield, along with side products resulting from cyclopropanation and selfcyclization (Scheme S2 of the Supporting Information). The reaction demonstrated a high tolerance of various functionalities, such as methyl (60), phenyl (61), halogens (62 and 63), and azide (64), on the vinyl unit of aryl allyl sulfides, furnishing target products in 51−85% yields. Sterically hindered 1,1-dimethylallyl aryl sulfide could still provide the desired product 65 in a high yield. Likewise, the current protocol was suitable for cyclohex-2-enyl aryl sulfide, giving desired product 66 in 49% yield with moderate diastereoselectivity (49% yield, dr = 7:2). Finally, the scope of this reaction was successfully extended to propargyl sulfides, thereby providing a convenient route to synthetically useful 1,4-enallenyl sulfides (Scheme 2B).<sup>21</sup> However, a slight modification from the original conditions, such as changing the base from NaH to CsF, is necessary to achieve a synthetically useful yield (see Tables S6−S9 of the Supporting Information). Under these modified conditions, aryl propargyl sulfides with methyl, isopropyl, tert-butyl, and chloro functionality at the para position of the aryl ring reacted smoothly to aford corresponding 1,4-enallenyl sulfides 67−71 in 50−58% yields.

Finally, a series of control experiments were conducted to elucidate the reaction mechanism (Scheme 3C). First, the reaction of vinyl triftosylhydrazone 1a with benzyl phenyl sulfide 77 failed to yield the Stevens rearrangement product 78 under standard conditions, which could rule out a probable [1,2]-sigmatropic rearrangement. Next, we performed reactions between vinyl triftosylhydrazone 1a and cinnamyl tolyl sulfide 79 with diferent Ag(I) catalysts. The diastereoselectivity (dr) of product 80 was consistently determined to be 2:1, regardless of the silver catalyst or its associated ligands. Based on the above observations and previous literature, this sivlercatalyzed [2,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement likely proceeds via a metal-free ylide intermediate (Scheme S4 of the Supporting Information).<sup>4c,e,g,8b</sup>
