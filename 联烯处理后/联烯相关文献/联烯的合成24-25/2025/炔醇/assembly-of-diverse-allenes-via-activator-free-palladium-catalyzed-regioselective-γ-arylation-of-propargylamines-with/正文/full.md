ABSTRACT: Controlling the reaction selectivity to achieve a precision synthesis is a constant concern for chemists. Here, we report a palladium-catalyzed deaminative coupling of propargylamines with arylboronic acids to generate allene skeletons. Importantly, this approach allows the regioselective γ-arylation of unactivated propargyl tertiary amines to access various allenes in the absence of amino-activating reagents. ## ■ INTRODUCTION

The amine family, a wide range of building blocks, has been extensively used as synthetic handles in synthetic chemistry and medicinal chemistry.<sup>1</sup> Although amines ofer immense synthetic leverage, the direct deaminative transformations of tertiary amines remain underutilized because the amino group, as a low heterolytic nucleofugal leaving group, has a high homolytic C−N bond dissociation energy and stronger characteristic basicity.<sup>2</sup> Accordingly, much efort has been spent toward the deaminative functionalization of unactivated tertiary amines. The classical method for C−N functionalization involves the conversion of tertiary amines to ammonium salts.<sup>3</sup> This preactivation strategy requires at least two distinct transformations to achieve C−N functionalization. In addition, in situ activation strategies such as the catalytic oxidation of tertiary amines to form iminium intermediates<sup>4</sup> or the use of activating agents<sup>5</sup> to induce deaminative functionalization have been demonstrated. However, each of these previous reports required the use of a large excess of oxidant or activator, producing chemical waste that does not meet the demand for green chemistry. Currently, many research groups have focused on a variety of transition metal-catalyzed<sup>6</sup> and metal-free strategies but are generally limited to aryl and benzylic tertiary amines in cross-coupling reactions. To date, a few examples of direct deaminative transformations of tertiary amines are stark reminders of the shortcomings in this area of synthetic chemistry. Therefore, developing a novel, eficient, and atomeconomic protocol for the C−N functionalization of tertiary amines is highly important.

On the other hand, allenes have proven to be eficient and versatile synthons in various cycloaddition reactions, providing access to valuable molecules with medicinal, agrochemical, and material significance.<sup>8</sup> They are also important scafolds widely found in natural products, bioactive compounds, and marketed drugs.<sup>9</sup> In this context, a great deal of eforts have been devoted to the development of eficient approaches for the construction of allene moieties.<sup>10</sup> In this regard, the transition metal-catalyzed deaminative reaction has emerged as one of the most attractive methods for the synthesis of allenes.<sup>11</sup> Among these deaminative strategies, the C−N cleavage of propargyl amines has been widely investigated, and various metal catalysts such as Zn, Cu, In, Cd, Ag, Au, and Pd have been employed (Scheme 1A).<sup>12</sup> However, these previous reports mainly rely upon the use of specific substrates and limit the structural diversity of allenes. The catalytic cross-coupling of electrophilic propargyl derivatives with various nucleophiles has ofered a promising approach for designing structural diversity and complexity.<sup>13</sup> However, the reported deaminative strategies to access allenes are limited to the utilization of propargyl ammonium salts and organometallic reagents through a process of γ-alkylation<sup>13b</sup> (Scheme 1B). To address this shortfall, difluorocarbene-induced and alkyne-mediated deaminative couplings of unactivated propargylamines and boronic acids have been successfully developed by Song's group<sup>14a</sup> and our group, <sup>14b</sup> respectively, yielding propargylic substitution products rather than allenes (Scheme 1C). The exploitation of catalytic deaminative coupling methods to access allenes using simple propargylamines and nucleophiles remains underdeveloped, with the main challenge being selective control to avoid the propargylic substitution reaction. As part of our ongoing investigation into propargylamine functionalizations, we report herein a novel palladiumcatalyzed deaminative coupling of propargyl tertiary amines and arylboronic acids to construct diverse allenes via a regioselective γ-arylation process (Scheme 1D).

## ■ RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

We initially discovered that the introduction of a substituent at the α-position of the nitrogen atom was highly efective in promoting the deaminative γ-arylation of propargylamines. Thus, we started our initial screen by using N,N-dimethyl-1,3- diphenylprop-2-yn-1-amine 1a and phenylboronic acid 2a as model substrates in the presence of $\mathrm { \bar { P d C l } } _ { 2 } ( \mathrm { P P h } _ { 3 } ) _ { 2 }$ (5 mol %), tributylphosphine (TBP; 10 mol %), and $\mathrm { K } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C O } _ { 3 } \left( 2 . 5 \mathrm { e q u i v } \right)$ in 1,2-dichloroethane (DCE; 2 mL) at $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for 3 h to aford allene product 3a in a 39% yield (Table 1, entry 1). Subsequently, various commercially available Pd catalysts including $\mathrm { P d } _ { 2 } ( \mathrm { d b a } ) _ { 3 } , \ \mathrm { P d } ( \mathrm { P P h } _ { 3 } ) _ { 4 } ,$ and Pd(OAc) were tested for this reaction (entries 2−4). To our delight, the yield of 3a was increased to 49% when $\mathrm { P d } ( \mathrm { P P h } _ { 3 } ) _ { \mathfrak { c } }$ was used. Diferent ligands were also examined in this coupling reaction under the catalysis of $\mathrm { P d } ( \mathrm { P P h } _ { 3 } ) .$ (entries 5−7), but none of them could improve the yield. In general, the trialkylphosphines $\left( \mathrm { P E t } _ { 3 } \right.$ and $\bar { \mathrm { P C } } \bar { \mathrm { y } } _ { 3 } )$ served as ligands and gave higher yields than those with an aromatic group such as 2-dicyclohexylphosphino- $^ { - 2 ^ { \prime } , 4 ^ { \prime } , 6 ^ { \prime } . }$ triisopropylbiphenyl (XPhos). Taking advantage of the addition of water to enhance the reaction,<sup>14b,15</sup> we then screened a number of solvents (fluorobenzene, trifluoromethylbenzene, toluene, and 1,4-dioxane) with the addition of water (entries 8−12). It is obvious that the combination of DCE and water is the most eficient for this deaminative transformation. We were pleased to find that the yield of allene 3a was further improved to 74% when the amount of phenylboronic acid 2a was increased to 2.5 equiv (entry 13). Lowering the reaction temperature to 100 ${ } ^ { \circ } \dot { \mathrm { C } }$ resulted in a significant decrease in the yield of 3a (entry 14, 56%). In addition, other experimental parameters such as the type and loading of the base, the amount of water added, and the reaction time were also evaluated, but all led to a negative result (for details, see the Supporting Information, Table S1).

With the optimized conditions in hand, we next investigated the generality of the reaction substrates (Scheme 2). First, we investigated the extent to which propargylamines 1 containing diferent $\mathbb { R } ^ { 1 }$ and R<sup>2</sup> substituent groups would react with phenylboronic acid 2a. Propargylamines 1 containing both aromatic and aliphatic $\mathbb { R } ^ { 1 }$ groups were smoothly converted to a small library of trisubstituted allenes 3ba−3ja in moderate to good yields. For example, various para-substituted aromatic groups, including $n – \mathrm { P r } , \mathrm { M e O } , \mathrm { C l } , \mathrm { B r } , \mathrm { F } ,$ and Ph substitutions as well as the thiophene group, all reacted smoothly. Analogously, aliphatic groups such as cyclohexenyl and cyclopropyl substitutions were also tolerated, providing the target products 3ia and 3ja in 74 and 58% yields, respectively. The generality of $\mathrm { R } ^ { 2 }$ groups on propargylamines 1 was then evaluated. In general, diferent R<sup>2</sup>-substituted propargylamines 1k−1p reacted well in this deaminative functionalization process. When the $\mathbb { R } ^ { 2 }$ substitution was the substituted phenyl groups, 44−88% yields of the desired products 3ka−3na were obtained. Other $\mathbb { R } ^ { 2 }$ groups including the n-butyl group and hydrogen could be converted to the desired allenes 3oa and 3pa in moderate yields. In addition, the modification of both $\mathrm { R } ^ { \hat { 1 } }$ and $\mathbb { R } ^ { 2 }$ groups was found to be eficient and aforded the corresponding products 3qa and 3ra in reasonable yields.

On the basis of the previous literature<sup>17</sup> and our studies, a plausible mechanism is proposed as shown in Scheme 5. First, the oxidative addition of Pd(0) species and propargylamine 1 forms the allenylpalladium complex A through σ−π−σ rearrangement. Subsequently, the allenylic palladium species A is converted to the Pd hydroxide intermediate B by reaction with water under basic conditions, followed by the transmetalation with boronic acid 2 to deliver the corresponding allenylic intermediate C. Finally, the allene product 3 is generated via reductive elimination of intermediate C and the catalytically active Pd(0) species is regenerated.

## ■ CONCLUSIONS

In summary, a straightforward synthetic protocol has been developed for the preparation of trisubstituted allenes via the palladium-catalyzed deaminative coupling of unactivated propargylamines and aryboronic acids. Importantly, we demonstrate for the first time that this catalytic deaminative coupling reaction can be achieved in the absence of any external activator. Compared to the traditional catalytic deaminative coupling via in situ amino activation, the activator-free method ofers some sustainable features in terms of operational simplicity, good functional group tolerance with high yield and regioselectivity, and the absence of toxic byproducts. Eforts to explore new synthetic applications for activator-free catalytic deaminative transformation are currently underway in our laboratory.
