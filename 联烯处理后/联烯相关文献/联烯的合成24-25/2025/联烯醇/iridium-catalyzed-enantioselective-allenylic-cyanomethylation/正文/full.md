ABSTRACT: The poor acidity of acetonitrile restricted its utility as a C2 synthon of a cyanomethyl group for incorporation into organic compounds. We now employed a 2-azido allylic alcohol as an acetonitrile enolate surrogate for the first enantioselective allenylic cyanomethylation. This mild and base-free protocol utilizes cooperative iridium and Lewis acid catalysis for the activation of branched allenylic alcohols to deliver α-allenylic acetonitriles in moderate to good yields with excellent enantioselectivity.

The attenuated acidity of the α-protons of acetonitrile compared to carbonyls (pK of CH CN of ∼31.3 in DMSO)<sup>3</sup> makes it relatively dificult to deprotonate, and thus, it remains inert toward electrophiles. In fact, the inertness of acetonitrile is a reason for its choice as a solvent in many chemical reactions. Although acetonitrile can be deprotonated using a strong base, the “hardness” and ambiphilic nature of the corresponding cyanomethyl anion makes its selective application as a carbon nucleophile challenging.<sup>4</sup>

Shibasaki and co-workers developed a general strategy for activating alkyl nitriles through a cooperative combination of Lewis acid with Brønsted base catalysis and accomplished enantioselective addition to a series of electrophiles.<sup>9</sup> In a recent study, Li et al. demonstrated a synergistic Rh and silane catalysis strategy to tame the reactivity of the cyanomethyl anion for enantioselective allylic substitution.<sup>10</sup>

Taking a completely diferent approach to overcome the challenges associated with the direct use of a cyanomethyl carbanion, Cui et al. in 2017 introduced 2-azido allylic alcohol 1 as an acetonitrile enolate surrogate (Scheme 1B).<sup>11</sup> This stable and easily accessible vinyl azide promises wide applicability due to its apparent compatibility with a variety of catalytic conditions for reactions proceeding under ionic or radical pathways (Scheme 2A, eqs 1 and 2).<sup>11,12</sup> To date, the only application of 1 in an enantioselective transformation is reported by Cao et al. in Ir-catalyzed allylic substitution (Scheme 2A, eq 3).<sup>13</sup>

To the best of our knowledge, no α- allenylation of alkyl nitriles (allenylic cyanoalkylation) has ever been reported, let alone an enantioselective variant. Herein, we present the first catalytic enantioselective allenylic cyanomethylation. 17

Although the Ir-catalyzed allenylic substitution reaction was first introduced by the Takeuchi group in 2004,<sup>18</sup> its enantioselective variant was not known until the seminal development by Carreira et al. through the application of their Ir(I)/(phosphoramidite,olefin) complex.<sup>19</sup> We envisioned the interception of η<sup>2</sup>-Ir(I)-bound allenylic carbocation intermediate A, proposed to be generated from a branched allenylic electrophile under cooperative Ir(I) and Lewis acid catalysis,<sup>19,20</sup> with 2-azido allylic alcohol 1 (Scheme 2B). The resulting α-allenylic iminodiazonium intermediate B was expected to spontaneously extrude nitrogen and acetone to generate α-allenylic acetonitrile.

To validate our catalytic hypothesis, we began our investigation with the model reaction between 2-azido allylic alcohol 1 and 1-phenyl allenyl alcohol 2a (Table 1). When the reaction was carried out in THF at $5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ using 6 mol % of an in situ generated Ir complex derived from [Ir(COD)Cl] and Carreira’s (P,olefin) ligand (S )-L1,<sup>21</sup> in the presence of 10 mol % $\csc ( \operatorname { O T f } ) _ { 3 }$ as the Lewis acidic promoter and 4 Å MS, the desired α-allenylic acetonitrile 3a was formed in 34% yield along with the homocoupled product 3a′ as a 1:1 inseparable mixture (entry 1). Despite the low yield, the high enantiomeric ratio (er) of 3a in this reaction was encouraging and called for further optimization. We have recently disclosed the homocoupling reaction of allenylic alcohols under divergent cooperative catalysis and shown it to proceed through the intermediacy of a cross-dienol intermediate generated via a 1,3- hydroxy transposition of allenylic alcohol.<sup>22</sup> We wondered whether the generation of cross-dienol could be avoided by replacing allenylic alcohol with allenylic carbonate 2a′. A reaction with 1.5 equiv of 2a′ under similar conditions aforded a slightly improved ratio of 3a over 3a′ (entry 2). A careful investigation revealed the formation of stable 4-phenyl 1,3- butadien-2-ol-carbonate (3a″) via [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement of 2a′ in this reaction. Although the use of 2.5 equiv of 2a′ improved the yield of 3a to 45%, a considerable amount of the homocoupled product was observed (entry 3). When the reaction was carried out at an ambient temperature in the presence of 2 equiv of allenylic alcohol 2a, α-allenylic acetonitrile 3a was isolated in 53% yield with 99:1 er and an improved 3a to 3a′ ratio (5.5:1) (entry 4). Changing the desiccant to 5 Å MS further enhanced the 3a/3a′ ratio, even though it was at the cost of the yield (entry 5). Considering the low nucleophilicity of 2-azido allylic alcohol 1 compared to the in situ generated cross-dienol, we anticipated that maintaining a low concentration of allenylic alcohols could be beneficial. Accordingly, when 2a was employed as the limiting substrate, the desired α-allenylic acetonitrile 3a was obtained almost exclusively (23:1 3a/3a′) and isolated in 61% yield with 99.8:0.2 er (entry 6). (R )-H -BINOL-derived (P,olefin) L2 instead of (S )-L1 aforded ent-3a in a poor yield with reduced er (entry 7), which shows the importance of the BINOL backbone in the (P,olefin) ligand for high yield and enantioselectivity. A slightly better reaction profile was observed using 5 Å MS as the desiccant when 3a was formed exclusively in 65% yield with excellent er (entry 9).

Having optimized the reaction conditions, we set out to showcase the generality of this enantioselective allenylation of 2-azido allylic alcohol 1. As shown in Table 2, allenylic alcohols bearing electron-rich as well as electron-deficient 1-aryl substituents were well-accommodated, and the corresponding α-allenylic acetonitriles (3a−3p) were obtained in moderate to good yields and generally with excellent enantioselectivity. Consistent with the proposed involvement of the allenylic carbocation (A in Scheme 2B) in this reaction,<sup>19</sup> allenylic alcohols containing an electron-rich aryl group generally resulted in products with higher yields compared to those bearing electron-deficient aryl substituents. Para- or meta substituted aryl rings on allenylic alcohol were found to be better suited in this reaction compared to the ortho-substituted ones. In the latter case, the products were obtained in lower yields, although excellent enantioselectivity was maintained. A low yield of 3m is in agreement with the lesser stability of the corresponding allenylic carbocationic intermediate as well as slow nucleophilic attack of 1, leading to faster decomposition of allenylic alcohol 2m. Allenylic alcohols containing a disubstituted electron-rich aryl group (2k and 2l) aforded the products (3k and 3l) in moderate to good yields with high er. Allenylic alcohols 2n−2p with polyaromatic substituents delivered the products (3n−3p) in moderate yield while maintaining outstanding enantioselectivities.

Allenylic alcohols substituted with heterocycles, such as 1,3- benzodioxolane (2q) and diferently substituted thienyls (2r and 2s), are compatible with our reaction conditions. However, the corresponding products $\left( 3 \mathbf { q } - 3 \mathbf { s } \right)$ were obtained in relatively low yields. Ferrocene-substituted allenylic alcohol 2t provided the α-allenylic acetonitrile in only 39% yield with 90:10 er. The lower enantioselectivity points toward the competing background reaction, likely due to the ease of formation and higher stability of the ferrocene-substituted free allenylic carbocation.<sup>23</sup> Aliphatic allenylic alcohols failed to participate in this allenylic cyanomethylation reaction, presumably due to the lower stability of the corresponding allenylic carbocation, which is a limitation of this catalyst system. It is noted that the formation of no other side products could be detected in any of these cases. The low yield of the product in certain cases (3o−3q) can be attributed to the decomposition of allenylic alcohol under Lewis acidic conditions.

To showcase the scalability of our protocol, a reaction between 2-azido allylic alcohol 1 and allenylic alcohol 2d was carried out on a 1.0 mmol scale under our optimized reaction conditions (Scheme 3A). The desired product 3d was isolated in 55% yield with 99.5:0.5 er. The synthetic utility of α- allenylic acetonitriles was illustrated through several synthetic transformations involving 3d (Scheme 3B). Selective reduction of the cyano group in 3d with DIBAL-H at $0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ aforded α- allenylic acetaldehyde 4 in 85% yield. This two-step process represents the first formal α-allenylation of acetaldehyde. The addition of the phenyl Grignard reagent to 4 followed by oxidation of the resulting unpurified secondary alcohol (with 1:1 dr) led to the formation of α-allenylic acetophenone 5 in 91% yield over two steps with 99.5:0.5 er. The absolute configuration of 5 was ascertained to be R by comparing its specific rotation to that of a sample obtained through α- allenylation of vinyl azide as a ketone enolate surrogate.<sup>16</sup> The absolute configuration of other α-allenylic acetonitriles shown in Table 2 was tentatively assigned as the same by analogy.

Reductive ozonolysis of 3d resulted in primary alcohol $\mathbf { 6 , }$ which was protected as carbamate 8. Reduction of 3d with $\mathrm { L i A l H } _ { 4 }$ and Boc protection aforded N-Boc bis-homoallenylic amine 9, which was subjected to Ag-catalyzed cyclization: 2,3- disubtituted pyrrolidine 10 was obtained as a single diastereomer in 43% yield over three steps with excellent enantioselectivity.<sup>24</sup> No erosion of the enantiopurity was observed in any of these reaction sequences. In summary, we have developed the first catalytic enantioselective allenylic cyanomethylation using 2-azido allylic alcohol as acetonitrile enolate surrogate.<sup>17</sup> This transformation proceeds through the cooperative activation of branched allenylic alcohol by an $\mathrm { I r } \big ( \mathrm { I } \big ) / \bar { \big ( } P , \mathrm { o l e f i n \big ) }$ complex and Lewis acidic $\dot { \mathrm { S c } } ( \mathrm { O T f } ) _ { 3 }$ and delivers α-allenylic acetonitriles in moderate to good yields with outstanding enantioselectivity. Easy accessibility of the substrates, base-free reaction conditions, and ambient temperature are some of the practical advantages of our protocol. Eforts toward the utilization of this acetonitrile enolate surrogate in other catalytic enantioselective transformations are currently underway in our laboratory.
