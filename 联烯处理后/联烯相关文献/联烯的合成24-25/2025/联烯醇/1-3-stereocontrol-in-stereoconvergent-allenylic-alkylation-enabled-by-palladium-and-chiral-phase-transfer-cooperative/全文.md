# 1,3-Stereocontrol in Stereoconvergent Allenylic Alkylation Enabled by Palladium and Chiral Phase-Transfer Cooperative Catalysis

Hongbo Qi,\* Wenhua Wu, and Dan-Xing Wu

ABSTRACT: A stereoconvergent allenylic alkylation of allenyl acetates with oxindoles has been established by palladium and chiral phase-transfer cooperative catalysis, allowing for an eficient construction of 1,3-nonadjacent stereogenic elements featuring both central and axial chirality. The noncovalent interactions of PTC create a chiral environment for nucleophilic attack on $\eta ^ { 3 } \mathrm { - }$ allylpalladium intermediates. Mechanistic investigations reveal that the Pd/PTC synergistic action and matched chirality are both critical for stereocontrol, while PTC primarily determines the stereoselectivity. Notably, this work represents the first successful implementation of Pd/chiral PTC dual catalysis for 1,3-nonadjacent stereocontrol, thereby enriching the synthetic toolbox for constructing challenging remote stereocenters.

A<sup>symmetric</sup> <sup>catalysis</sup> <sup>stands</sup> <sup>at</sup> <sup>the</sup> <sup>forefront</sup> <sup>of</sup> <sup>modern</sup> organic synthesis, enabling the stereocontrolled assembly of enantioenriched molecules vital to pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and functional materials.<sup>1</sup> The prevalence of multiple stereogenic elements in bioactive molecules<sup>2</sup> has made stereoselective construction a long-standing research focus in organic synthesis.<sup>3</sup> Despite remarkable advances in vicinal stereocenter construction, the precise control of nonadjacent stereocenters remains a persistent challenge.<sup>4</sup> Three interrelated factors contribute to this synthetic challenge:<sup>5</sup> (i) the flexible transition state emerges as a critical barrier to reliable remote stereocontrol; (ii) asymmetric induction eficiency declines with greater stereocenter separation; and (iii) nonadjacent stereocenter formation produces ≥4 stereoisomers, increasing synthetic complexity.

Chiral allenes represent a privileged class of structural motifs found in natural products, bioactive molecules and functional materials.<sup>6</sup> Consequently, the design of eficient synthetic routes to chiral allenes has become a major objective in organic synthesis, and significant advances have recently been achieved in the field of constructing single allenyl axial chirality. Transition-metal-catalyzed asymmetric allenylic alkylation represents a powerful strategy for the stereoselective construction of enantioenriched allene derivatives with precise stereocontrol.<sup>7d,8,9</sup> Notably, allenyl axial chirality with vicinal central chirality could be controllably installed using transitionmetal-catalyzed.<sup>8g,10</sup> However, the simultaneous control of remote stereocenters featuring both allenyl axial chirality and central chirality remains limited,<sup>8i,j,9</sup> despite the fact that such scafolds play a significant role in medicinal chemistry and synthetic chemistry.<sup>11</sup> In 2023, two distinct Pd/chiral amine dual catalyst systems were independently developed by Mi and Luo<sup>9e</sup> and Li and Shao<sup>9b</sup> for allylic allenylation reactions of β- ketocarbonyl and aldehyde, respectively, enabling the 1,3- nonadjacent stereocenters with allenyl axial chirality and central chirality to be stereoselectively controlled. Subsequently, Wen and Guo described a Pd/chiral aldehyde cooperatively catalyzed asymmetric allenylic alkylation of unprotected amino acid esters, providing an eficient access to these valuable scafolds (Scheme 1a).<sup>9f</sup> Beyond metal/ organocatalytic approaches, bimetallic catalysis has proven highly efective for precise stereocontrol in constructing these scafolds, as demonstrated by Zhang and Ma<sup>8j,9g</sup> and $\mathrm { H e } ^ { \mathrm { \tiny { 9 h } } }$ and $\operatorname { L i n . } ^ { 9 }$

Chiral PTCs are important type of organocatalysts and have grown to be indispensable tools for asymmetric synthesis, uniquely enabling stereocontrol of anionic nucleophiles via well-defined ion-pair interactions.<sup>12</sup> Notably, this system faces heightened background reactivity relative to classical organocatalysts. Additionally, PTC systems feature a single activation site, specifically a chiral cationic center that mediates ionic interactions, restricting the simultaneous activation of both reaction partners. This limitation prompted developing PTCbased dual catalytic systems with secondary activation modes, significantly broadening synthetic applications.<sup>13</sup> Since the Pd/ chiral PTC-catalyzed asymmetric allylic alkylation of Schif bases reported by Gong<sup>14a</sup> and Takemoto,<sup>14b</sup> transition metal/ chiral PTC combined catalysis has emerged as a highly promising strategy in asymmetric catalysis.<sup>15</sup> However, such catalytic systems have rarely been applied to simultaneously and stereoselectively construct 1,3-nonadjacent stereocenters (Scheme 1b). We herein report a Pd/chiral PTC-catalyzed enantioselective allenylic alkylation, simultaneously creating allenyl axial chirality and all-carbon quaternary stereocenters with excellent enantioselectivity and diastereoselectivity (Scheme 1c).

We initiated a systematic study of the Pd/chiral PTCcatalyzed asymmetric allenylic alkylation using racemic allenylic acetate 1a and oxindole 2a as model substrates (Table 1). Several commercially available ligands (L1−L4) were evaluated in the presence of an L-isoleucine-derived quaternary phosphonium salt (PTC-1). While these systems could deliver the desired allylic allenylation product 3aa, both the enantioselectivity and diastereoselectivity proved unsatisfactory (entries 1−4). The observed suboptimal stereocontrol may be attributed to competing background reactivity mediated by a single Pd/ligand catalytic cycle. Inspired by findings from Takemoto<sup>14b</sup> and Zi,<sup>15f</sup> the deliberate use of electronically more deficeint phosphite ligand L5 would be able to slow down the competing monocatalytic process. Remarkably, this approach not only substantially enhanced enantioselectivity but also significantly improved diastereoselectivity, albeit at the expense of the reaction yield (entry 5). Subsequently, screening of various phase-transfer catalysts (PTCs) identified PTC-3 as the optimal, demonstrating remarkable enhancement in the yield, diastereoselectivity, and enantioselectivity. These results strongly suggest a synergistic catalytic efect between ligands L5 and PTC-3, as evidenced by comparative evaluation of diferent PTC systems (entry 7). Base optimization (entries 11−15) identified NaOH as critical for maximizing both reactivity and selectivity in the L5/PTC-3 system, achieving peak performance (entry 14). Systematic optimization of other reaction parameters, including catalyst loading, base stoichiometry, and reaction time, found that increased yields and slightly improved stereoselectivity were obtained upon using slightly increased amounts of the palladium catalyst and chiral PTC (entries 16 and 17).

Omitting chiral PTC yielded almost racemic 3aa (ee <10%) with poor diastereocontrol (Scheme 4a, entry 3). The result underscores the dual role of chiral PTC in both phase-transfer activation and enantioselective nucleophile delivery. Configuration inversion of either L5 or PTC-3 significantly eroded diastereoselectivity (Scheme 4a, entries 4 and 5), demonstrating that chiral complementarity between the phosphine ligand and phase-transfer catalyst is essential for optimal stereocontrol in this dual catalytic system. This matched/mismatched phenomenon suggests cooperative transition-state organization involving both chiral elements. Notably, ent-PTC-3 generated products with inverted enantiomeric excess (ee) values. These observations collectively demonstrate that the phase tranfer catalyst serves as the primary determinant of the stereoselectivity in this transformation. To probe potential kinetic resolution efects, the reaction was performed by using enantiomerically pure (R)- and (S)-1a as substrates (Scheme 4b). Intriguingly, both enantiomers eficiently underwent the asymmetric alkylation, afording the products with the same configuration, in similar yields and with identical enantioselectivity. This observation suggests that the quick racemization allows both substrate enantiomers of 1a to be funneled into a single product stereoisomer, leading to the enantioconvergent nature.

The proposed mechanism involves two catalytic cycles, as illustrated in Scheme 5. In the PTC cycle, PTC+ transfers the base OH- from the solid base to the organic phase. The enol form of 2a undergoes deprotonation by the base, generating a tightly associated nucleophilic ion pair (I) with PTC<sup>+</sup>, along with one equivalent of water. In the pal-ladium catalysis cycle, oxidative addition of Pd(0) to ra-cemic allene 1a to form both η<sup>3</sup>-allylpalladium interme-diate (R)-II and (S)-II, which rapidly equilibrates via $\eta ^ { 1 } .$ -allylpalladium intermediate III. The (S)-II intermediate is kinetically favored over its (R)-II counterpart for nucleo-philic attack by the chiral ion pair I. This preferential re-activity pathway enables the highly stereoselective in-termolecular asymmetric remote alkenylation, ultimately leading to the formation of product 3aa with excellent stereocontrol. In summary, we have established a stereoconvergent enantioselective allenylic alkylation of oxindoles with allenyl acetates enabled by cooperative catalysis of the chiral palladium complex and phase-transfer catalyst (PTC), leading to the highly stereoselective construction of 1,3-nonadjacent stereogenic elements featuring both central and axial chirality. The key to successful stereocontrol is the use of PTC’s noncovalent interactions to establish a chiral environment for the stereoselective nucleophilic addition to the η<sup>3</sup>-allylpalladium intermediates. Mechanistic studies reveal that the synergistic cooperation between the palladium catalyst and PTC, along with their matched chirality, plays a critical role in enabling excellent reaction performance (Scheme 5). This work significantly expands the scope of PTC applications in metal/organo cocatalysis. We anticipate that this strategy will provide a general platform for the stereoselective construction of remotely functionalized chiral centers in more challenging contexts.
