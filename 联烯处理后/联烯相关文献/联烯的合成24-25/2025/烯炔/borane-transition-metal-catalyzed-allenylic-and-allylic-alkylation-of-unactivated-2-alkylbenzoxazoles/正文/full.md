ABSTRACT: Asymmetric functionalization of unactivated alkylsubstituted azaarenes at the α-position of the alkyl substituent is challenging because deprotonation of the weakly acidic α-C−H bonds requires a strong base. This requirement often impedes efective enantiocontrol by chiral catalysts. As a result, the few reactions that have been reported have narrow substrate scopes. Herein, we report a method for highly diastereo- and enantioselective allenylic alkylation reactions of unactivated 2- alkylbenzoxazoles with conjugated enynes, enabled by synergistic borane/nickel catalysis. The success of this method hinges on catalytic activation of the 2-alkylbenzoxazoles by a triarylborane,

In particular, previously unreactive acyclic 1,3-disubstituted allylic esters are rendered viable electrophiles, enabling the construction of products bearing two adjacent stereocenters.

## ■ INTRODUCTION

Azaarene moieties substituted with alkyl groups containing carbon stereogenic centers are common in pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and natural products.<sup>1</sup> Direct functionalization of azaarenes at the α-position of a linear alkyl substituent via deprotonation with a base and electrophilic trapping of the resulting carbanion is the most straightforward strategy for constructing such structures. Although non-enantioselective variants of these reactions that aford racemic products are well developed,<sup>2</sup> catalytic enantioselective counterparts have largely been limited to azaarenes activated by electron-withdrawing groups<sup>3−5</sup> (Scheme 1a), which can be readily deprotonated with a relatively weak base (e.g., DBU or NEt ). In contrast, deprotonation of unactivated azaarenes requires a strong base (e.g., <sup>n</sup>BuLi or lithium bis(trimethylsilyl)amide), use of which increases not only the likelihood of uncatalyzed background reactions but also the possibility of chiral catalyst decomposition. Notably, the few successful examples of catalytic asymmetric reactions involving unactivated azaarenes have primarily been metal-catalyzed allylic alkylation reactions, typically employing metal-π-allyl electrophiles generated in situ.<sup>6</sup> Other classes of electrophiles are rarely reported.<sup>7</sup> To overcome these limitations and expand the range of compatible electrophiles, alternative strategies are required.

Hydrometalation of conjugated enynes has been reported to generate exo-alkylidene-metal-π-allyl electrophiles that can be coupled enantioselectively with various nucleophiles to aford structurally diverse allenes in the presence of a chiral metal catalyst (Scheme 1b).<sup>3c,8</sup> However, reported examples of such coupling reactions are limited to relatively acidic pronucleophiles, which can be readily deprotonated by a relatively weak base to generate the corresponding nucleophile. The resulting protonated base may then shuttle the proton either to the metal catalyst, forming a metal hydride that subsequently hydrometallates the enyne,<sup>3c,8a,b,d,e</sup> or directly to the metalated enyne;<sup>8c</sup> both pathways ultimately aford the electrophile. In contrast, deprotonation of less acidic pronucleophiles requires stronger bases and generates proton-locked conjugate acids that are inefective at transferring the proton. This inhibition of electrophile formation renders unactivated alkyl-substituted azaarenes unsuitable as pronucleophiles in metal-catalyzed reactions with conjugated enynes.

Based on these analyses, we hypothesized that strategies to enhance the α-C−H acidity of unactivated alkylazaarenes would enable their deprotonation by a relatively weak base,

Notably, the Wasa group and our group recently developed various synthetic methods based on increasing the acidity of pronucleophiles by means of borane activation.<sup>7f,9</sup> In $\cdot ^ { 7 \mathrm { f } , 9 }$ particular, in a prior study, our group demonstrated that a borane catalyst not only can activate azaarenes but also remains catalytically competent despite strong coordination by the nitrogen atom of the azaarene.<sup>7f</sup> This finding can be attributed to steric hindrance around the borane center, which promotes frustrated Lewis pair−type behavior.<sup>10</sup> We thus envisioned that borane activation of azaarenes could be utilized to accomplish unprecedented allenylic alkylation reactions of unactivated azaarenes with enynes. Indeed, we herein report that the desired transformation can be achieved by using a previously unreported synergistic borane/nickel catalytic system (Scheme 1c). The reactions exhibit a broad substrate scope and high diastereo- and enantioselectivities. In addition, we found that borane activation can be used for overcoming the limitations of palladium-catalyzed asymmetric allylic alkylation reactions of azaarenes.

Having optimized the conditions for the allenylic alkylation reaction, we investigated its substrate scope (Table 2).

Replacement of the 2-ethyl group in 1a with <sup>n</sup>propyl, <sup>n</sup>pentyl, or phenethyl groups aforded products 3b−3d in good yields with excellent diastereo- and enantioselectivities. When $\mathring { R ^ { 2 } }$ was <sup>i</sup>propyl, the increased steric bulk led to diminished reactivity and diastereoselectivity, afording product 3e in 20% yield and 1.5:1 dr, while maintaining high ee values for both diastereomers. The use of 2-methylbenzoxazole also resulted in a low yield of the desired product (3f, 29%) along with a dialkylation byproduct in 10% yield (not shown in Table 2). In contrast, when the <sup>i</sup>propyl or cyclopentyl group was positioned one carbon further along the alkyl chain, the reactions proceeded smoothly to give 3g and 3h in high yields. Furthermore, 2-alkyl chains bearing terminal ester, trifluoromethyl, phenoxy, acetoxy, or benzoyloxy substituent were well tolerated, afording products 3i−3m in 54−94% yields with excellent diastereo- and enantioselectivities (up to >20:1 dr and 99% ee). The diastereo- and enantioselectivity remained high across a range of benzoxazoles bearing diverse aryl ring substituents at various positions (3n−3v). Notably, benzoxazoles bearing ester substituents (1q and 1t) failed to furnish the corresponding products (3q and 3t) under conditions without LA1, indicating that the ester group could not provide the same activation as the borane catalyst. Aryl-substituted enynes bearing various electron-donating or electron-withdrawing groups at the ortho, meta, or para position of the benzene ring were compatible with the reaction conditions; products 4b−4h were obtained in 65−89% yields with high diastereo- and enantioselectivities. 2-Naphthyl-, 3- thienyl-, and alkyl-substituted enynes also underwent the reaction, afording target products 4i−4l with high diastereoand enantioselectivities. The absolute configuration of 4m was determined by X-ray crystallography of a single crystal. The scope of this reaction is presently confined to benzoxazoles (see the Supporting Information for incompatible heteroarenes).

The allenylic alkylation reaction was readily scaled up: the reaction of 1a (5.0 mmol) with 2l (10.0 mmol) aforded 4l in 84% yield with 11:1 dr and 99% ee (Scheme 2). Desilylation of 4l with tetrabutylammonium fluoride provided 5 in 82% yield. Subsequent gold-catalyzed intramolecular cyclization<sup>3c,12</sup> of 5 via hydroxyl attack on the allene furnished 6 in 78% yield as the E-olefin exclusively, with retention of both dr and ee. In contrast, N-iodosuccinimide-mediated cyclization<sup>13</sup> of 5 gave 7 in 90% yield with the Z-olefin as the major isomer $\left( Z / E \right. =$ 6.3:1), also with retention of dr and ee.

Subsequent deprotonation by triethylamine gives a borane-ligated carbanion (or aza-enolate),<sup>14</sup> which acts as a nucleophile in the coupling reaction. In the nickel cycle, the alkyne moiety undergoes oxidative addition to Ni(0) to generate a three-membered Ni(II)−olefin ring,<sup>8c</sup> which is subsequently protonated by the conjugate acid of the base to furnish a vinylnickel species. This species undergoes a rapid σ−π rearrangement to generate the key exo-alkylidene-Ni(II)- π-allyl electrophile. Outer-sphere attack by the nucleophile on the electrophile gives the product,<sup>15</sup> simultaneously regenerating the borane and nickel catalysts.

Inspired by the success of synergistic borane/nickel catalysis of allenylic alkylation reactions of unactivated 2-alkylbenzoxazoles, we explored the applicability of catalytic systems involving borane and other transition metals to asymmetric allylic alkylation reactions of unactivated azaarenes, which remain challenging, particularly for the construction of products with two adjacent stereogenic centers. To date, only two examples of the formation of compounds with vicinal stereocenters have been reported,<sup>6a,b</sup> both of which are restricted to cyclic allylic esters�likely because the steric hindrance associated with acyclic systems decreases their reactivity. We hypothesized we could overcome this challenge of poor reactivity of acyclic allylic esters by tuning the borane catalyst.

Through optimization of the reaction conditions (see Supporting Information), we found that a borane/palladium system eficiently catalyzed asymmetric allylation of 2- alkylbenzoxazoles with racemic 1,3-disubstituted acyclic allylic pivalates to form products with vicinal stereocenters (Table 3). Furthermore, using 1.0 equiv of LA4 without TESBr resulted in a 60% yield of 9a (see Supporting Information). Because the pivalate anion coordinates more strongly than bromide to the borane catalyst, owing to the better interaction of the oxygen 2p lone pair with the empty boron 2p orbital, TESBr may enhance catalyst turnover by scavenging the pivalate anion after product formation.

## ■ CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, we used synergistic borane/transition metal catalysis to overcome the challenges posed by asymmetric functionalization of unactivated alkylazaarenes at the α- position of the alkyl substituent. This strategy allowed us to achieve the first examples of coupling reactions between unactivated 2-alkylbenzoxazoles and conjugated enynes, which were promoted by a borane/nickel catalytic system and aforded previously elusive compounds with 1,3-nonadjacent stereocenters. Moreover, diastereo- and enantioselective asymmetric allylic alkylation reactions of unactivated 2- alkylbenzoxazoles with acyclic 1,3-disubstituted allylic esters were achieved with a borane/palladium system. A key element of both transformations is the use of fluorinated triarylboranes, which enable base-mediated deprotonation of otherwise poorly acidic α-C−H bonds under mild conditions. The method has a broad substrate scope, allowing reactions of diverse 2- alkylbenzoxazoles and electrophiles (>60 examples). Our work not only provides powerful solutions for accessing complex chiral architectures bearing multiple stereocenters from readily available precursors but also establishes synergistic borane/transition metal catalysis as a versatile strategy for asymmetric functionalization reactions.
