# Enantioselective synthesis of isoxazolines bearing allenes by palladium-catalyzed carboetherification of β,γ-unsaturated ketoximes with propargylic acetates†

Feifei Jin,<sup>a</sup> Cangzhu Hu,<sup>a</sup> Shuaijie Wu,<sup>a</sup> Guodong Zhang, <sup>a</sup> Ying Han,<sup>a</sup> Chao-Guo Yan, <sup>a</sup> Wenbo Li,\*<sup>b</sup> Chaoshen Zhang\*<sup>c</sup> and Lei Wang \*<sup>a</sup>

Received 11th December 2024, Accepted 23rd January 2025

DOI: 10.1039/d4qo02319f

A highly eficient Pd-catalyzed enantioselective carboetherification reaction of β,γ-unsaturated ketoximes with propargylic acetates is demonstrated. This method provides various chiral isoxazolines bearing multisubstituted allene groups in good yields with excellent enantioselectivities. The sterically electron-rich BINOL-derived phosphoramidite ligand exhibits highly eficient control over both chemoselectivity and enantioselectivity. The salient features of this reaction include readily available starting materials, broad functional group tolerance, an easy scale-up, versatile transformations and promising photo-physical properties. DFT calculations were carried out to disclose the detailed mechanism and origins of the enantioselectivity.

## Introduction

Over the past decades, transition-metal-catalyzed carboheterofunctionalization reactions of alkenes have become a powerful method for the construction of heterocyclic compounds.<sup>1–3</sup> In this regard, palladium-catalyzed carboetherification of alkenes with aryl/alkenyl halides allows the construction of a new C–C bond and a new C–O bond in a single reaction, which has greatly expanded its scope.<sup>4</sup> Despite significant progress, the development of such an asymmetric reaction still remains a challenge, and only a few strategies have been explored so far. For instance, Mazet and co-workers developed an elegant Pdcatalyzed intermolecular carboetherification of dihydrofurans, which provided access to a variety of chiral-fused tetrahydrofurobenzofurans (Scheme 1a).<sup>5</sup> On the other hand, Pd-catalyzed enantioselective carboetherification of alkenes bearing a nucleophilic moiety with aryl/alkenyl halides was also developed.<sup>6–10</sup> For example, the first palladium-catalyzed enantioselective carboetherification reaction of alkenols to aford chiral tetrahydrofuran was realized by Wolfe in 2015 (Scheme 1b).<sup>6</sup> Later, Tang’s group developed enantioselective palladium-catalyzed alkene aryloxyarylation to give a series of chiral $^ { 1 , 4 \cdot }$ -benzodioxanes, chromans, and $^ { 1 , 4 }$ -benzooxazines.<sup>8</sup> Recently, the group of Zhang reported an eficient Pd/Xiang-Phos catalyzed carboetherification of alkenyl oximes to aford isoxazolines with good enantioselectivity.<sup>9</sup> Despite remarkable progress, however, these electrophiles in the carboetherification reaction are limited to aryl/alkenyl halides. With regard to the significance for increasing the diversity of compounds, developing new electrophile variants and widespread applications of specialized structural units would be highly valuable.

Allenes are key structural motifs in natural products and also serve as potential precursors in the synthesis of many bioactive molecules, drugs and target molecules.<sup>11,12</sup> Consequently, the development of novel methodologies to synthesize allenes has attracted enormous attention.<sup>13</sup> Among many strategies, high progress has been achieved in the transition-metal-catalyzed cross-coupling of propargyl electrophiles with various nucleophiles for the preparation of allenes.<sup>14</sup> Remarkably, Zhou and co-workers developed an eficient palladium-catalyzed asymmetric Wacker-type coupling of threecomponent allenylation reactions.<sup>15</sup> Very recently, our group developed a novel palladium-catalyzed asymmetric alleneamination of $\beta , \gamma \cdot$ -unsaturated hydrazones with propargylic acetates, which provides facile access to the installation of various multisubstituted allene groups onto chiral dihydropyrazoles.<sup>16</sup> In continuation of our interest in the allenylation reaction, we are curiously interested in whether the reaction might be extended to an asymmetric version of carboetherification of alkenyl oximes with propargylic acetates for the synthesis of allenyl substituted chiral isoxazolines. To achieve this carboetherification reaction, several side reactions must be prevented, and several intricate challenges should be conquered: (1) how to eficiently control the isomerization of $\beta , \gamma \cdot$ -unsaturated oximes to α,β-unsaturated oximes and prevent propargylic substitution with nucleophilic oximes; (2) minimize β-hydrogen elimination of propargylic acetate atoms to give byproduct enynes; and (3) how to sequentially and eficiently control both chemoselectivity and enantioselectivity in one pot. Herein, we describe an eficient palladium-catalyzed enantioselective carboetherification of alkenyl oximes with propargylic acetates, delivering enantioenriched isoxazolines bearing allenyl groups in good yields with excellent enantioselectivities (Scheme 1c).

At the onset of our study, 2-methyl-4-phenylbut-3-yn-2-yl acetate 1a with β,γ-unsaturated oximes 2a were selected as the model substrates to optimize this asymmetric carboetherification reaction. We envisioned that our previously developed palladium-catalyzed asymmetric alleneamination of β,γ-unsaturated hydrazones with propargylic acetates would be capable of controlling the stereochemistry of this asymmetric carboetherification reaction. However, we were disappointed to not observe even traces of the desired product 3a by using Pd $( \mathrm { c o d } ) \mathrm { C l } _ { 2 }$ as the catalyst, L1 as the ligand, and $\mathbf { K } _ { 2 } \mathbf { C O } _ { 3 }$ as the base in CPME (cyclopentyl methyl ether) at 50 °C under an argon atmosphere (Table 1, entry 1). Screening of bisphosphine ligands (L2 and L3) and P-chiral ligand L4 led to the desired product 3a in poor yields with low enantioselectivities (Table 1, entries 2–4). We then investigated the TADDOLderived chiral phosphite ligand L5, and the desired product 3a was obtained in 55% yield with a poor ee value (Table 1, entry 5). It was found that BINOL-derived phosphoramidites (L6 and L7) failed to improve yields and enantioselectivities (Table 1, entries 6 and 7). On further screening more sterically hindered ligands (L8 and L9), we found that L9 was the best ligand, afording 3a in 82% yield with 89% ee (Table 1, entry 9). With the best ligand (L9) in hand, we then focused on exploring various palladium sources. The efect of palladium sources such as Pd<sub>2</sub>(dba)<sub>3</sub>, Pd(OAc)<sub>2</sub> and allylpalladium(II) chloride failed to improve the yield and enantioselectivity of 3a (Table 1, entries 10–12). However, the substrate has relatively good reactivity in THF and toluene, leading to 3a with a relatively lower ee value (Table 1, entries 13 and 14). Notably, a range of bases such as NaO<sup>t</sup>Bu, $\mathrm { { K O } } ^ { t } \mathrm { { B u } } ,$ , and $\mathbf { N a } _ { 2 } \mathbf { C O } _ { 3 }$ did not give good results (for additional details, please see the ESI†). Finally, the enantioselectivity could be further improved to 92% ee by the use of CPME (5.0 mL) at $5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ under argon for 48 h.

## Results and discussion

With the optimized conditions in hand, the scope of propargylic acetates 1 was first examined as shown in Scheme 2. For example, a variety of propargylic acetates bearing electrondonating or electron-withdrawing groups at the para position of the phenyl ring were all well tolerated, and aforded the desired products 3b–3h in moderate to excellent yields with 91–92% ees. Notably, substrates with aryl groups bearing ortho- and meta-substituents were also applicable to give the desired products 3i and 3j in 71–78% yields with 90–91% ees.

Moreover, 2-naphthyl propargylic acetates could be employed to deliver the corresponding product 3k in 81% yield with 91% ee. The absolute configuration of 3k was confirmed by X-ray crystallography analysis.<sup>17</sup> Heteroarene and 1-ethynylcyclohex-1-ene substituted propargylic acetates also proved to be good substrates in this transformation, afording 3l and 3m with comparable enantioselectivities. In addition, the propargylic carbonates containing five-, six- and eightmembered cyclic alkyl scafolds reacted smoothly to provide 3n–3q in moderate yields with excellent enantioselectivities (86–94% ees). Substrates with the ethyl or n-butyl located on the tertiary carbon atom of the propargylic acetates were also good reaction partners and led to the corresponding products 3r and 3s in good results.

Next, we then investigated the scope of unsaturated ketoximes in this asymmetric reaction. A wide range of β,γ-unsaturated ketoximes with phenyl rings containing electron-donating or electron-withdrawing groups at diferent positions were tolerated well, providing the desired products 4a–4g in 58–87% yields with 91–94% ees. Moreover, polycyclic aryl, heteroaryl and alkyl-substituted ketoximes could be employed to aford 4h–4k in moderate yields with good enantioselectivities (88–93% ees). In addition, gem-disubstituted alkenes ketoximes were successful in producing 4l–4o in excellent yields with 87–91% ees. However, replacing the methyl groups with other alkyl groups led to 4p and 4q in acceptable yields with relatively lower enantioselectivities of 77–78% ees.

To demonstrate potential applications, late-stage modification of pharmaceuticals and natural products was then conducted (Scheme 3). The clofibrate and fenofibrate derivative products 4r and 4s were obtained in moderate yields with high ees. In particular, estrone menthol, carbohydrate and cholesterol derived 4t–4w were aforded in good yields with high diastereoselectivities.

To further explore the utility of this protocol, we carried out experiments to probe a 5 mmol scale reaction of 1a under the standard conditions, and the desired product 3a was obtained in 78% yield (1.18 g) with 92% ee (Scheme 4a). Moreover, the enantioenriched isoxazolines can be transformed into β-hydroxy ketones 6 in 60% yield with 91% ee. ## Conclusion

In summary, we have developed a novel Pd-catalyzed enantioselective carboetherification reaction of β,γ-unsaturated ketoximes with propargylic acetates to aford various chiral isoxazolines bearing multisubstituted allene groups in good yields with excellent enantioselectivities. This reaction ofers advantages including a general substrate scope, broad functional group tolerance, good yields and high enantioselectivity. These highly enantioenriched products are precursors of other useful chiral building blocks. The photo-physical properties were further investigated, indicating their potential application in organic optoelectronic materials. DFT calculations further indicated that the stereochemistry was regulated by the chiral ligand-promoted olefin insertion step, wherein the favored transition state is stabilized by less steric hindrance in the chiral ligand and the substrates. Further expansion of the scope of asymmetric Pd-catalyzed reactions is ongoing in our laboratory.
