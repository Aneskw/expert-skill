# Lewis Base-Catalyzed Interhalogenation of Terminal Allenes: Selective Strategy for Accessing Vicinal Vinylic, Allylic Heterodihalides

ABSTRACT: Though precedent remains limited, the selective interhalogenation of allenes ofers a valuable synthetic strategy to access products where each halide exhibits orthogonal reactivity. Here, we describe a Lewis base-catalyzed approach for the dihalogenation (bromochlorination, iodochlorination, iodobromination, and dibromination) of terminal allenes. By employing just 1 mol % of triphenylphosphine oxide or hexamethylphosphoramide to activate thionyl halides in the presence of electrophilic halogenation reagents, we achieve the conversion of monosubstituted allenes to vicinal allylic, vinylic dihalides with up to 93% yield and >20:1 regioisomeric ratio, favoring the branched dihalogenated product. A range of functional groups is tolerated, including nitrile, ester, phosphate, sulfonamide, and silyl groups, and the reaction proved to be scalable. The utility of various dihalide products was investigated in substitution and cross-coupling chemistry, highlighting the distinct reactivity among the diferent classes.

Consequently, much progress has been made over the past decade, including recent work by our group, for the preparation of heterodihalides from alkenes and alkynes (Figure 1a).<sup>5,6</sup>

By comparison, the interhalogenation of allenes is hardly developed, despite recent advances in their selective functionalization (Figure 1b).<sup>7</sup> Further, the utility of the resulting heterodihalide products is apparent when one considers the divergent reactivity profiles innate to allylic and vinylic halides (e.g., nucleophilic displacement, cross coupling chemistry, etc.) and the broad utility of alkenes as synthetic handles. For these reasons, vicinal vinylic, allylic heterodihalides, though underexplored, bear the capacity to serve as prized synthetic lynchpins.

Strategies for allene interhalogenation are generally plagued by issues of regio-, stereo-, and site-selectivity.<sup>7e,8</sup> The onerous nature of these transformations is exemplified in the limited number of reports detailing the bromochlorination of allenes (Figure 1b). Two independent examples disclosed the use of

BrCl for the bromochlorination of propadiene or 2,3- pentadiene.<sup>9</sup> However, these reactions sufer from low yields and the presence of solvent-incorporated byproducts. In some cases, solvolysis represented the major reaction pathway or no yields were reported altogether, raising concerns as to the overall eficiency of this transformation. During the course of our studies, Katayev and co-workers disclosed a unique transfer dihalogenation strategy applicable to allenes. However, only a singular example of vicinal bromochlorination on an arylsubstituted allene was demonstrated.<sup>10</sup>

We sought to develop a strategy for the interhalogenation of terminal allenes by utilizing sulfinyl halides, thereby permitting catalyst control in the availability of the nucleophilic halide anion (Figure 1c). Based on our prior work and the studies of others, we surmised that a transient methylenehaliranium species (II) could be intercepted by a halide anion salt (III) generated through Lewis base activation of thionyl chloride or thionyl bromide.<sup>6,11,12</sup> This strategy would allow for the precise installation of two diferent halides across the three consecutive sp<sup>2</sup>-hybridized carbons of an allene to furnish dihalogenated product 2. To date, common methods employed for allene dihalogenation are either unselective or primarily feature linear product (3) selectivity with varying levels of stereoselectivity.<sup>13,14</sup>

We commenced our investigations by surveying conditions for allene bromochlorination. When 4,5-hexadienylbenzene (1a) was subjected to thionyl chloride and a slight excess of Nbromosuccinimide (NBS) in the absence of a catalyst, only trace quantities of isomeric bromochlorides 2a and 3a were observed (Table 1, entry 1). Instead, a significant amount of undesired dibromination byproduct was observed. Under the same conditions, but in the presence of 1 mol % triphenylphosphine oxide, product was formed in 82% yield, favoring branched bromochloride 2a with a 13:1 regioisomeric ratio (rr) (entry 2).

While various solvents were generally suitable, trifluorotoluene proved optimal with respect to reaction eficiency and selectivity (entries 2−4).<sup>15</sup> A survey of Lewis base catalysts revealed triphenylphosphine oxide and hexamethylphosphoramide (HMPA) as superior catalysts, delivering up to 85% yield (18:1 rr) at minimal loadings (entries 5−8). In the examination of alternative bromenium sources, N-bromophthalimide (NBP) provided optimal results, particularly when used in combination with HMPA, afording bromochloride 2a in 93% yield with 17.5:1 rr (entries 9−11). Further, the reaction displays similar eficiency under noninert conditions (entry 12).

With the optimized conditions established, we next examined the scope of this transformation (Table 2). In all cases, compound 2 was obtained as the major product, with varying levels of linear-to-branched regioselectivity. Monosubstituted allenes, both with and without alpha-branching substituents, provided similar regioisomeric ratios (rr), yielding branched dihalide products (2a, 2b, and 2d). Furthermore, the reaction demonstrated scalability, allowing for the isolation of 2.9 g of 2a with minimal loss of eficiency and selectivity. One exception, aryl allene 1c, resulted in both poor yield and selectivity, likely due to conjugation modulating the reactivity of the internal alkene. However, this strategy enabled the synthesis of the branched bromochloride product 2c as the major isomer, notably in contrast to previous methods that typically favor the formation of linear dihalide products.

Allenes bearing a variety of functional groups were welltolerated regardless of their proximity to the reactive site (2e− 2m).<sup>16</sup> In all cases, the desired products were obtained in good-to-high yields with generally high selectivity. Though many products were isolated with improved yield and/or selectivity using the HMPA/NBP conditions (2a, 2b, 2j, and 2m), some substrates (e.g., 1h and 1i) performed better with the OPPh /NBS conditions instead. Furthermore, substrate 1k, which contains a Lewis basic phosphate substituent, did not require the addition of a catalyst.

By modifying the electrophilic and nucleophilic halogenating reagents to N-iodosuccinimide (NIS) and SOBr or NIS and SOCl , a range of iodobrominated and iodochlorinated products were isolated (2n−2u), albeit with reduced selectivity. For comparison, optimization substrate 1a yielded iodobromide 2n in 87% yield with a 4:1 rr and iodochloride 2r in 88% yield with a 4.7:1 rr. Notably, although allene 1u, derived from the uric acid reducer probenecid, formed a crude product mixture with a 1.5:1 ratio of 2u to 3u, the branched product could be cleanly isolated, yielding iodochloride 2u as a pure isomer in 50% yield.

Historically, methods for the dibromination of allenes have been plagued by poor yields and/or selectivity.<sup>13b,17</sup> Therefore, we were interested in evaluating the selectivity of this catalytic manifold by comparison. Using NBP with thionyl bromide under the standard catalytic conditions developed here, the dibromination of allenes 1v−1y largely favored halogenation at the internal alkene, including substrates featuring substructures derived from marketed therapeutics (1w, 1y). These results demonstrate, in some cases, the orthogonal selectivity imparted by this catalyst-controlled strategy (see Supporting Information for additional examples).

To investigate the utility of these uncommon vicinal vinylic, allylic heterodihalides in organic synthesis, we chose to explore the innate reactivity diferences between each class of dihalide (Scheme 1).<sup>18</sup> Focusing initially on bromochlorides 2a and 2b, we applied copper-catalyzed $S _ { \mathrm { N } } \dot { 2 } ^ { \prime }$ allylic alkylation, yielding 4a in 62% with an E/Z ratio >10:1.<sup>19</sup> In contrast, $S _ { \mathrm { N } } 2$ displacement proved more challenging, though acetate 4b was obtained in low yield.<sup>20</sup> To illustrate the utility of these products for consecutive functionalization, enolate alkylation to aford vinyl bromide 4c followed by Stille cross-coupling gave an overall two-step yield of 53% of ester 4d.

Comparatively, the analogous iodochloride 2r demonstrated poorer selectivity both in the copper-catalyzed allylic alkylation (4e) and the direct substitution with KOAc (4f). A limitation of the iodohalides, in general, is their bench instability, necessitating rapid purification of the dihalide followed by immediate setup for subsequent diversification reactions. Nevertheless, iodobromide 2n exhibited superior $S _ { \mathrm { N } } 2 ^ { \prime }$ reactivity with p-thiocresol (4g, 80% yield 1:5.7 E/Z) followed by copper-catalyzed amide coupling of the vinyl iodide to aford enamide 4h in 59% isolated yield.<sup>21,22</sup>

As anticipated, vicinal vinylic, allylic dibromides proved to be the best compromise between the bromochlorides and the iodohalides, owing to the lability of the allylic bromide combined with the better stability of the vinyl bromide. Probenecid-derived dibromide 2y could undergo highly selective regiodivergent substitutions to aford vinyl bromides 4i and 4j in synthetically useful yields. Notably, palladiumcatalyzed Tsuji-Trost substitution with benzenesulfinate was feasible without significant β-halo elimination to the allene, as observed with other dihalide variants.<sup>23</sup>

In conclusion, we have developed a catalytic dihalogenation of monosubstituted allenes, leading to vicinal vinylic and allylic dihalides. Products were isolated in up to 93% yield and >20:1 regioselectivity using common electrophilic reagents in combination with thionyl halides as latent nucleophiles activated by simple Lewis base catalysts. This convenient strategy for the synthesis of bromochlorides, iodochlorides, iodobromides, and dibromides employs noncryogenic reaction temperatures and low catalyst loadings of 1 mol %. The resulting dihalides were evaluated as useful building blocks for the preparation of a diverse range of functionalized di- and trisubstituted alkenes.
