Such processes represent an unprecedented reaction pathway for cobalt catalysis that enables simultaneous and precise incorporation two molecules of aldehydes onto the more sterically congested alkene of the allenes and construction of three stereogenic centers, delivering a range of enantioenriched 1,4-diols in up to 77% yield, >98:2 dr, and 98:2 er.

## Scheme 1. Background and Reaction Design

![](images/f7e9db12ac54880850ae9c56c804fa563e845c566cf13dfbd140691afd5243cb.jpg)

T <sup>ransition</sup> <sup>metal-catalyzed</sup> <sup>enantioselective</sup> <sup>coupling</sup> <sup>of</sup> unsaturated hydrocarbons and aldehydes provides a straightforward and modular approach for the construction of chiral alcohols without the need for prefunctionalization. Compared with reactions of unsaturated hydrocarbons with a single reactive site such as alkynes, multiple reactive sites of allenes complicated the chemo-, regio-, and stereoselectivity issues. Therefore, transformations of allenes have remained rare and more challenging. Significant progresses on catalytic enantioselective coupling of allenes and aldehydes promoted by Ir-based,<sup>2</sup> Ru-based,<sup>3</sup> Cu-based,<sup>4</sup> Pd-based,<sup>5</sup> and Rh-based<sup>6</sup> catalysts have been made (Scheme 1a). Such reactions proceeded through sequential nucleophile−metal addition to allenes followed by enantioselective reactions of the resulting allyl−metal species with aldehydes. Based on the reaction mechanism, only one molecule of aldehyde was able to be fused with one molecule of allene, enabling the formation of a single hydroxyl group. In contrast, the simultaneous incorporation of two molecules of electrophilic aldehydes onto allenes to generate enantioenriched diols in a cascade process remained unknown. Enantioenriched diols are important skeletons of various natural products, drugs, and chiral building blocks in organic synthesis. The development of eficient and stereoselective methods to construct enantioenriched diols is thus sought after in both academia and industry. Compared with multiple strategies for catalytic enantioselective synthesis of 1,2- and 1,3-diols, access to enantioenriched 1,4-diols containing remote stereogenic centers relied on catalytic enantioselective hydrogenation of 1,4-diketones, generating two stereogenic centers by C−H bond-forming reactions (Scheme $1 \check { \boldsymbol { \mathsf { b } } } ) . ^ { 7 }$ However, catalytic enantioselective preparation of 1,4-diols through carbon−carbon bond-forming reactions has remained elusive.

An alternative pathway for reactions of allenes and aldehydes is oxidative cyclization. Oxidative cyclization of two π-bonds promoted by low-valent transition metal complexes represents a classical elementary step in organometallic chemistry. Transition metal-catalyzed transformations through oxidative cyclization constitute one of the most important approaches for carbon−carbon bond-forming reactions in organic synthesis. Although protocols for catalytic enantioselective coupling of various unsaturated hydrocarbons and aldehydes/imines through oxidative cyclization have been reported, only one molecule of electrophilic aldehydes/imines could be incorporated (Scheme 1c).<sup>8</sup> In contrast to reactions of alkynes and 1,3-dienes, we developed the only example of cobalt-catalyzed divergent enantioselective 1:1 coupling of allenes and aldehydes through oxidative cyclization.<sup>9</sup> For 1:2 transformations of unsaturated hydrocarbons with electrophiles, pioneering work on cobalt-catalyzed enantioselective sequential coupling of $^ { 1 , 3 \cdot }$ -enynes with two molecules of ethylene was described by RajanBabu and co-workers (Scheme 1d).<sup>10</sup> Subsequently, our group developed a cobalt-catalyzed protocol for enantioselective divergent coupling of 1,3-enynes with two molecules of acrylates.<sup>11</sup> In both cases, two carbon− carbon bonds were formed at the alkyne moiety. Connecting two molecules of aldehydes to unsaturated hydrocarbons and incorporating two molecules of electrophiles onto an alkene moiety remained unknown. We imagined that taking advantage of our previous work on cobalt-catalyzed regiodivergent and enantioselective coupling of 1,1-disubstituted allenes and aldehydes,<sup>9</sup> the metallacycle (I, II, or III) containing an allyl−cobalt unit generated from oxidative cyclization of allenes and aldehydes might undergo allyl addition to the second molecule of aldehyde, enabling double functionalization of the more sterically congested alkene of the allenes (Scheme 1e). However, such a process is challenging. One of the key issue is chemoselectivity. The lower-lying LUMO of allenes led to a higher afinity for the metal center, and inherently, allenes have higher reactivity. Scarce examples of Rh-catalyzed and Cocatalyzed 2:1 coupling of allenes and aldehydes have been reported.<sup>12</sup> Moreover, an alcoholic solvent and water are required for catalyst turnover. Competitive protonation of the allyl−cobalt intermediate must be suppressed. In addition, a single chiral catalyst has to precisely control the regio- and stereoselectivity of the oxidative cyclization and aldehyde addition processes. Herein, we present an unprecedented protocol for chemo-, regio-, and enantioselective cascade reductive coupling of 1,1-disubstituted allenes with two molecules of aldehydes to aford a broad scope of 1,4-diols containing three stereogenic centers that are otherwise dificult to access.

We commenced our studies with the reaction of 1,1- disubstituted allene 1a with benzaldehyde 2a in the presence of various Co complexes (Table 1). Zn powder was required as the stoichiometric reductant. With the optimal conditions in hand, we investigated the scope of allenes (Scheme 2). 1,1-Disubstituted allenes bearing electron-deficient (3b−d and 3l), electron-rich (3g−k, 3n, and 3o), halogenated (3d−f, 3l, and 3m), and sterically congested (3g and 3o) aryl groups underwent the cascade 1:2 reductive coupling, afording the enantioenriched 1,4-diols in 37−68% yields with 76:24−92:8 dr and 94:6−98:2 er for the major diastereomer. Pyridyl-substituted allene was well tolerated (3p). Functional groups such as ester (3c) and amine (3h) are compatible with the reaction conditions. Reaction of silylsubstituted allene delivered 1,4-diol 3q in 58% yield with >98:2 dr albeit 73:27 er for the major diastereomer. Allenes substituted with two alkyl groups could be transformed into the desired 1,4-diols (3r−t) in 31−63% yields with 86:14 to >98:2 dr and 83:17−93:7 er for the major diastereomer. Bisphosphine 6f was optimal for the transformation of commercially available dimethyl-substituted allene without the need for the addition of water, furnishing 1,4-diol 3u in 77% yield with >98:2 dr and 89:11 er. It should be mentioned that in some cases (3c, 3d, 3h, 3i, and 3o), diastereomers could be separated by silica gel chromatography. Monosubstituted allenes could not be converted into the desired products. Oligomerization of allenes occurred preferentially.

We next explored the scope of the aldehydes (Scheme 3). Only aryl aldehydes could participate in the cascade reaction. Aliphatic aldehydes were unreactive under the reaction conditions. A variety of aldehydes containing electron-rich (7a, 7d, 7e, and 7h−m), electron-deficient (7c and 7g), halogenated (7c, 7g, 7l, and 7m), and sterically congested (7a and 7i−m) aryl groups could be converted into the desired 1,4-diols in 34−77% yields with 83:17−92:8 dr and 92:8−98:2 er for the major diastereomer. Functional groups such as silyl (7b), thiol ether (7e), and styrene (7f) are quite compatible. Although sizable 3,4-disubstituted benzaldehydes were able to be transformed into the desired 1,4-diols (7j−m), aryl aldehydes bearing a substituent at position 2 were unreactive. Reactions of heteroaryl aldehydes (7n and 7o) delivered the enantioenriched 1,4-diols in 37−48% yields with 81:19−86:14 dr and 91:9−97:3 er for the major diastereomer.

The cascade reaction can be performed on a gram scale (Scheme 4a). Transformation of 1,1-disubstituted allene 1b (1.037 g) with benzaldehyde 2a (2.228 g) promoted by the Co complex derived from 6i furnished 1,4-diol 3d (1.580 g) in 64% yield with 93:7 dr and 95:5 er. Directed epoxidation of the 1,1-disubstituted alkene moiety induced by VO(acac) enabled highly diastereoselective construction of other stereogenic centers in 95% yields (Scheme 4b).<sup>13</sup> Cyclization of 1,4- diol 3d aforded P-stereogenic phosphoramidites 9 and 11 in 64% and 43% yields with 86:14 and 82:18 dr, respectively (Scheme 4c,d).<sup>14,15</sup> In particular, phosphoramidite 11 could be isolated with 95:5 dr, showing its potential as a new class of Pstereogenic phosphoramidite ligands.

To gain some mechanistic insight, we conducted a series of experiments (Scheme 5). Increasing the amount of water to 10 equiv led to complete suppression of the cascade 1:2 coupling reaction (Scheme 5a). Competitive 1:1 coupling product 4a was formed exclusively. Such a result indicated that 3a and 4a were generated via the same metallacycle intermediate. The stereochemical control of the oxidative cyclization was not exclusive, causing diastereomers after the second aldehyde addition. Removal of water from the reaction conditions resulted in a significant decrease in the reaction eficiency, suggesting that water was crucial for the second aldehyde addition and/or catalyst turnover (Scheme 5b). Transformation of allene 1a in the absence of aldehyde 2a provided a complex mixture of self-oligomerization products, implying that the catalyst accurately controlled the chemoselectivity of the initial oxidative cyclization process (Scheme 5c). Parallel isotope kinetic experiments illustrated that cleavage of the O− H bond of water might be the rate-determining step (Scheme 5d).

Based on all of the experimental observations and literature precedents, we propose the possible catalytic cycle (Scheme 5e). Oxidative cyclization of allene 1a and benzaldehyde 2a promoted by Co(I) complex II in situ generated from Co(II) precursor I aforded Co(III) metallacycle III bearing an allyl− Co(III) moiety, which was able to undergo allyl isomerization via π-allyl−Co(III) intermediate IV to aford more stable allyl−Co(III) complex V containing a tetrasubstituted alkene. The 1,3-allylic strain in VI led to a more favorable formation of intermediate V. One-electron reduction of Co(III) metallacycle V to Co(II) species VII followed by allyl addition to benzaldehyde 2a and subsequent protonation/exchange with a Zn salt released product 3a. The second allyl addition might be accelerated by detaching the OH group and assisted by Zn salt and water (VII → VIII). Reduction of the Co(II) species with Zn regenerated the catalyst.

In conclusion, we have disclosed an unprecedented protocol for chemo-, regio-, diastereo-, and enantioselective cascade coupling of 1,1-disubstituted allenes and aldehydes promoted by a Co complex derived from a commercially available bisphosphine ligand, afording a broad scope of enantioenriched 1,4-diols that are otherwise dificult to access and enabling simultaneous construction of three stereogenic centers. The synthetic utility was demonstrated by functionalization of the products, delivering densely functionalized 1,4- diols and P-stereogenic phosphoramidites. Mechanistic studies revealed a novel cascade reaction pathway for cobalt catalysis. Such discoveries unveiled new opportunities for designing new cobalt-catalyzed enantioselective reactions and pushing forward the development of organocobalt chemistry. Further investigations of other cobalt-catalyzed enantioselective cascade transformations are underway.
