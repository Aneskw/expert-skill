However, in comparison to the elegant success for the synthesis of 1,1-dithioethers and 1,2-dithioethers,<sup>8,9</sup> the direct difunctionalization of alkenes to access $^ { 1 , 3 \cdot }$ -dithioethers has not been developed, which is mainly due to the lack of an appropriate dithiolation system. Considering the significant importance of 1,3-dithioethers in medicinal chemistry, developing an eficient method for their synthesis is of great value.

Dithioacetals are versatile synthons frequently used as the mask of carbonyl groups as well as valuable synthetic precursors for certain organosulfur compounds in transition metal catalysis.<sup>10,11</sup> The reactions involving insertion into the carbon−sulfur bond of dithioacetals provide a privileged platform for the rapid construction of functionalized dithioether compounds. Palladium-catalyzed thiol group migrations, which involve the formal insertion of unsaturated 2π units into the carbon−sulfur bond of dithioacetals, have already been developed (Scheme 2a).<sup>12</sup> However, these transformations were limited to only intramolecular processes. Recently, isolated intermolecular cases had also been illustrated with isocyanides or diazo compounds.<sup>13</sup> Nevertheless, the substrate scopes were still restricted to C1 sources, resulting in only 1,2-dithioether compounds rather than the desired 1,3- dithioethers.

Our group had previously demonstrated the feasibility of the intermolecular aminomethylamination of allenes with aminals to synthesize 1,3-diamines (Scheme 2b).<sup>14</sup> Building upon this, we became particularly interested in exploring the potential of using dithioacetals as thiomethylthiolation reagents to construct $^ { 1 , 3 \cdot }$ -dithioethers. Herein, we successfully implemented this idea and developed a palladium-catalyzed insertion of allenes into dithioacetals to access $^ { 1 , 3 }$ -dithioether compounds (Scheme 2c). Distinguished from previous dithiolation processes, which solely depended upon the carbon−sulfur bond formation, this method allowed for the simultaneous construction of one carbon−carbon bond as well as one carbon−sulfur bond. It should also be noted that alkyl sulfur sources, which could hardly be tolerated in preceding dithiolation reactions, were well amendable to this protocol. This method ofered an expedient solution for the rapid construction of a variety of structurally diverse $^ { 1 , 3 \cdot }$ -dithioethers, which can be used as valuable synthons and pharmacophores ubiquitously found in numerous natural products.

complex Pd−A with 1.5 equiv of dithioacetal 2a in dichloromethane (DCM) at room temperature rapidly produced dithioacetal-coordinated cationic palladium complex Pd−B (eq 1 in Scheme 7). The solid structure of Pd−B was unambiguously characterized by X-ray crystallographic analysis. Subsequently, the standard reaction proceeded smoothly in the presence of Pd−B, furnishing the desired 1,3-dithioether product 3a in 56% yield (eq 2 in Scheme 7). Furthermore, after 10 min of the standard reaction, we performed the high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) analysis of the reaction mixture (eq 3 in Scheme 7), which detected that alkenyl palladium Pd−C was the main palladium species (see the Supporting Information for details).

On the basis of the aforementioned mechanistic studies and previous reports,<sup>16</sup> we proposed a plausible mechanism for the palladium-catalyzed thiomethylthiolation of allenes with dithioacetals (Scheme 8). Initially, ligand exchange took place to form dithioacetal-coordinated cationic palladium complex Pd−I, which was considered to be the resting state of this transformation. Upon the decoordination of one thioether motif of dithioacetal, the terminal carbon−carbon double bond of allene was able to coordinate to the cationic

palladium complex to form Pd−II. Subsequently, nucleophilic attack of the decoordinated thioether motif to activated allene formed alkenyl palladium Pd−III, which was exactly detected by HRMS analysis. In the meantime, sulfonium species 4a was formed via carbon−sulfur bond cleavage. Finally, migratory insertion of sulfonium 4a into the C(sp<sup>2</sup>)−Pd bond took place, yielding product 1,3-dithioether and regenerating cationic palladium complex Pd−I through dithioether exchange. The nucleophilic attack of the C(sp<sup>2</sup>)−Pd bond to sulfonium 4a also made it possible to complete the catalytic cycle.

In summary, we developed novel palladium-catalyzed thiomethylthiolations of allenes with dithioacetals, allowing for the rapid synthesis of a wide range of 1,3-dithioethers. A unique dithioacetal-coordinated cationic palladium complex was isolated and characterized, which was considered the crucial palladium intermediate for this transformation. Further application and the detailed mechanism of this transformation are currently ongoing in our laboratory and will be reported in due course.
