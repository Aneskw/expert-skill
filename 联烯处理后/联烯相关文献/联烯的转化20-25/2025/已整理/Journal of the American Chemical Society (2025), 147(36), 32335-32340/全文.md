ABSTRACT: Two methods, designed primarily for applications in diversity-oriented synthesis (DOS), are introduced. The first is a Cu−H-catalyzed multicomponent process that involves a nitrile and a trisubstituted allenyl boronate. The first set of products are homoallylic α-secondary NH -amines, containing a trisubstituted alkenyl boronate, formed with >98% enantiospecificity, in up to >98:2 dr and as a single alkene isomer (>98% Z). The second method is eficient and diastereoselective triflic anhydride-mediated conversion of an unsaturated azapane-amide, generated from modification of the products of the foregoing catalytic process to azabicyclooctanes (ABCOs). The ABCOs contain a bridged enamine moiety and a trisubstituted alkene, which can be functionalized chemo- and stereoselectively. Accordingly, natural product-like rigid bicyclic frameworks can be obtained wherein four diferent fragments can be installed in space in a controllable fashion.

This would allow topological optimization of a molecule’s receptor binding.

In planning these studies, the inspiration came from azapenes (AZPs; Scheme 1a), exemplified by benazepril<sup>4</sup> and the related azabicyclooctanes (ABCOs), such as crinine,<sup>5</sup> lyconadin A<sup>6</sup> and himgaline.<sup>7</sup> We wanted to develop methods that can be used to generate stereochemically defined multifunctional compounds that, while possessing the same AZP or ABCO core framework, would be clearly distinct from any extant natural products. The first step would be a catalytic multicomponent reaction that afords a multifunctional core platform. A related transformation, reported previously, is promoted by a Cu−B(pin) and a Cu−H catalyst (Scheme 1b)<sup>8</sup> and involves a nitrile and a monosubstituted allene (→ Cuallyl-1 → ketim-1 → amin-1). Here, we would have a chiral trisubstituted allenyl boronate (Scheme 1c) and a Cu−H catalyst<sup>9</sup> would be involved in both C−C bond formation (→ Z-ketim-2) and ketimine reduction (→ Z-amin-2). Because the catalytic reaction would deliver a Z-trisubstituted alkenyl boronate (vs a 1,1-disubstituted boronate in the earlier report), we would be able to access AZPs via Z-amin-2 (azp; Scheme 1c). Ensuing amide formation (amid) and cyclization through a keteniminium salt (ketenim)<sup>10</sup> by a Bischler−Napieralskitype cyclization would deliver ABCOs bearing a rarely encountered<sup>11</sup> bridgehead enamine and a fourth stereodefined moiety (enam-1−4).

Our plan raised several questions. Compared to reactions with allenes bearing a single substituent, where a Cu−H complex adds to the less substituted site, the situation is more complex with a trisubstituted allenyl boronate. Although Cu− allyl-2 formation would be favored based on electronic stabilization,<sup>12</sup> addition to the less substituted C�C would be less sterically demanding. It was also unclear to what degree stabilization of electron density<sup>12</sup> by the boryl unit would impact the allyl-metal’s nucleophilicity, and whether it would be this initially formed species that would react with a nitrile, a transformation that, as far as we know, has no precedent, or a less substituted Cu−allyl complex. There would be no internal C�N→B chelation to facilitate Cu−H addition, and the ketimine would be more exposed and susceptible to a second Cu−allyl addition. 8 Furthermore, in view of the dynamic nature of Cu−allyl ,<sup>12</sup> we did not know to what degree Z-amin-2 would be favored (vs E). It was also unclear how eficient and diastereoselective ketimine reduction would be without C� N→B coordination. However, the criteria are diferent when diversification is the primary objective (applicable to lead identification) as opposed to the more traditional selectivity/ target-oriented synthesis (relevant to process optimization). Regio- and diastereoselective dihydroxylation<sup>18</sup> of 13a to generate 20 is a representative case (Scheme 5d), which underscores the twisted enamine’s electron-deficient character. These enamines resist reduction, likely because the exo aryl moiety would engender severe steric repulsion if the alkene were to be hydrogenated.

In closing, two methods are introduced for potential DOS applications. The first is a catalytic enantiospecific and diastereoselective multicomponent process that generates the core platform: homoallylic amines containing a stereodefined trisubstituted alkenyl boronate. The second is a $\mathrm { T f } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ -mediated cyclization that converts the derived AZPs to $_ \mathrm { A B C O s }$ bearing a twisted bridgehead enamine. Assorted chiral N-containing frameworks can thus be synthesized in which up to four fragments can be controllably positioned in space. Unlike studies related to $\mathrm { C } \big ( \mathrm { s p } ^ { 3 } \big )$ -rich phenyl bioisosteres,<sup>19</sup> the frameworks generated here are not replacement skeletons with moieties that are positioned the same way as the parent systems.
