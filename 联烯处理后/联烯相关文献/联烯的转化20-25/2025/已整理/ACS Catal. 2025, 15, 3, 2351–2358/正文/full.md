ABSTRACT: Axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes are an important type of stereoisomer, resulting from the perpendicular arrangement of two pairs of substituents. Despite the considerable progress made in the catalytically enantioselective synthesis of atropisomers, the available methods for accessing axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes have been underutilized. In this study, we

describe a copper-catalyzed enantioselective synthesis of axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes using acyl allenols. The axial chirality is established through a central to axial transfer via an enantioenriched allylcopper species. This reaction features mild conditions, >30:1 regioselectivities, and up to 98% ee. Control experiments reveal that the bulky size of the leaving group has a significant influence on the enantioselectivity of the reaction. The alkene and vinylboronate groups of the products provide versatile platforms for further transformations to enrich the structural diversity of products.

KEYWORDS: copper catalysis, enantioselective desymmetrization, axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes, acyl allenols, allenylic substitution

A<sup>xially</sup> <sup>chiral</sup> <sup>compounds</sup> <sup>have</sup> <sup>received</sup> <sup>significant</sup> attention from both synthetic and medicinal communities due to their widespread presence in natural products, chiral ligands, and catalysts.<sup>1</sup> Among these, the catalytical enantioselective synthesis of (hetero)aryl-based C−X (X = C, O, N, B, etc.) atropisomers<sup>2</sup> and allene-based atropisomers<sup>3</sup> has been a hot topic in the synthetic community over the past few decades (Scheme 1a). In comparison, the enantioselective synthesis of axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes remains in its infancy due to the challenge arising from the longer distance between the four groups about the chirality axis. At present, the majority of methodologies to access enantioenriched axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes focuses on the noncatalytic strategy, i.e., with the aid of stoichiometric chiral reagents.<sup>4</sup> The catalytic methods in this area have been somewhat limited,<sup>5</sup> including enantioselective olefination of 4-substituted cyclo hexanones and their analogs,<sup>6</sup> desymmetrization of 4- substituted cyclohexanes and derivatives,<sup>7</sup> [2 + 2] cycloaddition between terminal alkenes and allenes,<sup>8</sup> and deracemization of racemic alkylidenecycloalkanes<sup>9</sup> (Scheme 1b). In view of the remarkable roles of axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes in bioactive molecules and chiroptical molecular switches as well as chiral ligands (Scheme 1c),<sup>10</sup> further development of novel methods for synthesizing these architectures is still highly desirable.

α-Allenols make up a crucial class of allenes that possess a 1,2-propadiene moiety and a hydroxyl group. Due to their π- activated reactivity, α-allenols and their derivatives have found extensive applications in organic synthesis,<sup>11</sup> and various fascinating transformations have been well-established, whereinto, transition-metal catalyzed C1-allylic substitution has emerged as a general strategy to construct allenes bearing a central chirality,<sup>12</sup> or chiral allenes<sup>13</sup> (Scheme 2a, upper panel). When the external nucleophiles attack the C3-position of 2,3- allenol and analogues via transition-metal catalysis, achiral multiply substituted 1,3-dienes would be generated (Scheme 2a, bottom panel).<sup>14</sup> However, to the best of our knowledge, the enantioselective construction of axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes using α-allenols and their derivatives remains unknown. We hypothesized that by rational design of allenol derivatives and precise control of the regioselectivity, it would be possible to generate axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes by enantioselective C3-substitution.<sup>15</sup> Building upon our research interests in transition-metal-catalyzed enantioselective desymmetrization and atroposelective construction of axially chiral compounds,<sup>16</sup> we present herein a copper-catalyzed enantioselective allenylic substitution of acyl 2,3-allenols (Scheme 2b). With phenylsilane used as the hydride donor, the axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes containing a 1,3-dienyl motif are constructed in good yields with excellent enantioselectivities. In addition, copper-catalyzed enantioselective silylation and borylation could also proceed, giving products containing functionalized dienyl motifs.

When allenol 2a was used, desired product 6a could only be obtained in 29% yield with 34% ee (entry 8). Switching acetyl allenol 1a to benzoyl allenol 3a resulted in a significantly reduced yield, albeit comparable enantioselectivity (entry 9, 39% yield with 73% ee). Introducing a bulkier acyl group led to a marked improvement in the enantioselectivity of 6a, with ee values of 93−96% and yields of 73−80% (entries 10 and 11). We also observed that the choice of solvent greatly influenced the yield of the reaction (entries 12−14). Among the solvents tested, toluene exhibited the best performance, generating compound 6a in 81% yield with 97% ee. Furthermore, upon scaling up the reaction to a 0.5 mmol level, there has been a slight enhancement in both the yield and the enantioselectivity of product 6a (entry 15).

With the optimized conditions established, we next explored the substrate scope of acyl allenols, as presented in Table 2. The pivaloyl allenols derived from 4-arylcyclohexanones, which bear electron-donating or electron-withdrawing groups at the para-, meta-, or ortho-positions of the phenyl ring, all proved suitable for this transformation, delivering 6b−i in 74−93% yields with 96−97% ee. Substrates carrying a piperonyl, 2- naphthyl, or heteroaryl group at the C4-position produced products 6j−q in 68−96% yields with 92−98% ee. Introduction of various alkyl groups led to products 6r−t in 70−82% yields with 97−98% ee. Products 6u−y, containing a series of heteroatom-based group such as BnO-, PhMe Si-, $\mathrm { A r } _ { 2 } ( \mathrm { O } ) \mathrm { P } \mathrm { - }$ , or PhSe- group, were successfully synthesized in 61−85% yields with 92−97% ee. Notably, the relative configuration of substrates has a great efect on the enantioselectivity, as well as the absolute configuration of the products. With trans-5u used as the substrate, ent-6u was isolated in 91% yield with 79% ee. Furthermore, 4,4- disubstituted cyclohexyl pivalates were also compatible with this transformation, resulting in the formation of product 6z in 76% yield with 88% ee and 6aa in 75% yield with 91% ee. Product 6ab containing an internal tetra-substituted olefin motif could also be obtained in 78% yield, albeit with lower enantioselectivity. Substrates meso-5ac and meso-5ad were able to render products 6ac and 6ad in 67−79% yields with 84− 90% ee. Notably, this transformation allowed for the simultaneous construction of three chiral elements, namely, two C-stereogenic centers and one axial chirality, in a single step.<sup>2a</sup> To enrich the product diversity, a series of cyclobutanone-derived pivaloyl allenols were also examined. Substrates containing phenyl rings with electron-donating or electron-withdrawing groups attached to the cyclobutanol backbones were found to be compatible with the reaction conditions, afording 6ae−aj in 70−90% yields with 90−94% ee. It is worthy of noting that all reactions proceeded with excellent regioselectivities (rr > 30:1) and no allene byproduct was detected.

To gain some insights into the reaction mechanism, several control experiments were conducted. As shown in Scheme 3a, with silyl allenol ether 7a used, the corresponding product 6a was isolated in 22% yield with 98% ee, accompanied by the formation of partially hydrogenated byproduct 8a in 32% yield. The excellent enantiopurity of 6a indicates that the bulky Osubstituent is essential to the enantioselectivity, while the acyl group governs the reactivity of substrates to form the chiral axis. At this stage, two possible mechanisms to generate the 1,3-dienyl motif could be proposed including β-oxy elimination from allyl copper INT-I (path a)<sup>17</sup> and Peterson olefination<sup>18</sup> from INT-II (path b) (Scheme 3b, upper panel). Replacing PhSiH with HBpin, product 6a could still be obtained in 71% yield with 98% ee (Scheme 3b, bottom panel), indicating that Peterson olefination (path b) was not involved. In addition, deuterated product 8a−D was isolated in 71% and 69% yields with >95% D in the presence of 2.0 equiv $\mathrm { D } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ or 4.0 equiv MeOH-d , hinting that allylcopper species INT-III was involved (Scheme 3c).<sup>19</sup> Moreover, with pivaloyl allenol 9a containing two methyl groups used, only trace product 10 was detected (Scheme 3d), which again hints that hydrocupration prefers to occur at the terminal position of allene motif.

Combined with previously reported literature,<sup>20</sup> a possible mechanism is proposed in Scheme 3e. First, CuH species is generated from CuOAc in the presence of L3 and PhSiH . Hydrocupration of 5a provides allylcopper INT-IV or INT-V. These two allylcopper species could undergo interconversion through a fast 1,3-copper shift.<sup>20,21</sup> β-Oxy elimination of INT-V will provide 6a and precatalyst LCu(OPiv). In this step, the axial chirality would be constructed via the central-to-axial chirality transfer. The high enantioselectivity of the substitution of 5a with PhSiH in the presence of (R)-DTBM-Segphos (L3) was rationalized with two possible transitionstate isomers, namely, models A and B (Scheme 3f). For both models A and B, a repulsion between the pivaloyl group of 5a and t-Bu group on the aryl unit of (R)-DTBM-Segphos (L3) is involved. In model A, there is a severe repulsion between the vinyl group of the substrate and the sterically bulky t-Bu group on the aryl unit of (R)-DTBM-Segphos (L3). In comparison, the repulsion between the hydrogen atom of substrate 5a and ligand L3 is much weaker in model B. Finally, σ-bond metathesis between LCu(OPiv) and PhSiH generates the LCuH species to enter the next catalytic cycle.

In summary, we have developed a copper-catalyzed enantioselective synthesis of axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes with acyl 2,3-allenols. This reaction features broad substrate scope, mild conditions, and excellent regio- and enantioselectivities. Control experiments show that the choice of acyl group is important to both the yield and enantioselectivity. The terminal alkene and vinyl boronate motifs of the products could participate in a series of downstream transformations. Further application of this catalytic strategy to the enantioselective synthesis of functionalized axially chiral alkylidenecycloalkanes is ongoing in our laboratory.
