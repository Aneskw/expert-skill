ABSTRACT: Herein, we report a method for enantioselective vinylogous addition of enones to alkoxyallenes enabled by synergistic borane/palladium catalysis. The inductive efect provided by borane coordination to the ketone was essential for closing the gap between the conditions needed for the generation of a dienolate and those needed for initiation of the palladium catalytic cycle by protonation of the metal catalyst. Furthermore, we accomplished the first example of stereodivergent synthesis with chiral borane/ transition-metal catalysts.

pronucleophile (NuH) is a versatile, atom-economical way to generate allylic compounds, which are widely used for the synthesis of natural products as well as drugs and other bioactive molecules.<sup>1</sup> As shown in Scheme 1a (top panel) with allenes as an example, the first step in these reactions is in situ formation of a M−H species by protonation of the metal catalyst with a proton generated by dissociation of the pronucleophile or a Brønsted acid cocatalyst. The resulting M−H species undergoes a hydrometalation reaction with the unsaturated hydrocarbon to generate a M(π-allyl) complex, which participates in an addition reaction with the nucleophile to deliver the allylic product. Since the pioneering work in this area by the groups of Trost,<sup>2</sup> Yamamoto,<sup>3</sup> and Breit<sup>4</sup> with palladium and rhodium catalysts, highly enantioselective reactions involving various pronucleophiles have been developed.<sup>1,5</sup>

A variety of carbonyl pronucleophiles have been used for this purpos e.<sup>2b−e,3a,4e,6</sup> Because both a proton and an enol or enolate nucleophile are required for such reactions, carbonyl pronucleophiles have been confined to compounds with relatively acidic α protons, which can form an enol nucleophile under acidic conditions and/or can dissociate to generate a proton when an acid cocatalyst is not present (Scheme $^ { \mathrm { 1 a , } }$ bottom panel). It can be imagined that if a weakly acidic carbonyl compound was used, access to the corresponding enolate would require a strong base, which would limit the availability of a free proton for protonation of the metal catalyst. To date, the only known reactions of poorly acidic carbonyl compounds, such as arylalkylketones and amides, have involved dienes and nickel catalysis.<sup>7</sup> Unfortunately, in palladium- and rhodium-catalyzed reactions, a proton captured by a strong base has dificulty reacting with the metal. To date, the least acidic carbonyl pronucleophile that has been used for the coupling reaction is 1,3- dimethylindolin-2-one ,<sup>2e</sup> the calculated $\mathsf { p } K _ { \mathrm { a } }$ of which in DMSO is 19.5, as determined by a machine learning approach developed by the Luo group. 8,9

Enolization of $\alpha { , } \beta { \cdot }$ -unsaturated carbonyls has enabled their vinylogous addition reactions with various electrophiles.<sup>10,11</sup> However, vinylogous coupling reactions of $\alpha { , } \beta { \cdot }$ -unsaturated carbonyls with allenes, 1,3-dienes, or alkynes have not been reported.<sup>12</sup> This challenge arises from the weak acidity of the γ-protons. For enones (e.g., 1a with a calculated $\mathsf { p } K _ { \mathrm { a } }$ of 25.8 in $\mathrm { \dot { D M S O } ) , ^ { 8 } }$ enolization can be accomplished with potassium bis(trimethylsilyl)amide (KHMDS).<sup>13</sup> However, bis-(trimethylsilyl)amine has a $\mathsf { p } K _ { \mathrm { a } }$ of 30 in $\mathrm { D M S O } , ^ { 1 4 }$ so proton dissociation is strongly disfavored (Scheme 1b). Recently, our group developed a method for asymmetric vinylogous Mannich reactions between enones and imines with catalysis by a chiral bicyclic bisborane.<sup>15,16</sup> Coordination of the borane catalyst with the carbonyl oxygen inductively increases the acidity of the γ-proton of the enone, facilitating enolization by a trialkylamine. The steric bulk of the catalyst prevents mutual quenching with the amine base. Such an unquenched borane− amine pair can be described as a frustrated Lewis pair, as defined by Stephan.<sup>17</sup> Because protonated triethylamine has a measured $\mathsf { p } K _ { \mathrm { a } }$ of 9.0 in DMSO,<sup>14</sup> which is acidic enough to protonate a palladium catalyst,<sup>18</sup> we hypothesized that a synergistic borane/palladium system might be able to promote addition reactions between enones and allenes in the presence of a trialkylamine proton shuttle. Indeed, we herein report that we have developed a method for highly regio- and enantioselective coupling reactions between enones and alkoxyallenes mediated by a synergistic catalyst system and $^ { 1 , 2 , 2 , 6 , 6 }$ -pentamethylpiperidine (PMP) as the base (Scheme 1c).

We began by investigating the reaction between enone 1a and allene 2a (Table 1). After careful screening of the reaction conditions, we found that target product 3a could be obtained in 96% yield with 92% enantiomeric excess (ee) by reaction of 0.20 mmol 1a with 0.30 mmol 2a in 1.0 mL of $\mathrm { P h C F } _ { 3 } \mathrm { a t } - 1 0$ °C for 24 h in the presence of 10 mol % $\mathrm { B } ( \mathrm { C } _ { 6 } \mathrm { F } _ { 5 } ) _ { 3 } ,$ 10 mol % CpPd(allyl), 22 mol % ligand L1, and 1.0 equiv of PMP (entry 1). The strong Lewis acidity and steric bulk of $\mathrm { B } ( \mathrm { C } _ { 6 } \mathrm { F } _ { 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ were critical, as indicated by the fact that $\scriptstyle \mathrm { \mathrm { B } } ( 2 , 4 , 6 -$ $\mathrm { F } _ { 3 } \mathrm { C } _ { 6 } \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } ) _ { 3 } ,$ which is less acidic,<sup>19</sup> and $\mathrm { B F } _ { 3 } { \cdot } \mathrm { O E t } _ { 2 } ,$ which is less bulky, were inactive (entries 2 and 3). The metal Lewis acids Sc(OTf) Yb(OTf) and Mg(OTf) were also inactive (entries 4−6). Variation of the ligand revealed that phosphoramidite ligands L2 and L3 promoted the reaction; L4, (S)-BINAP (L5), and Trost’s ligand (L6) failed to aford the desired product (entries 7−11). Next, the efect of the base was studied. The bulky base diisopropylethylamine (DIPEA) gave results similar to those obtained with PMP (96% yield and 90% ee, entry 12). The less bulky bases Et N and N-methylpiperidine gave lower yields and ee values (entries 13 and 14), perhaps because of coordination of the base to the borane or palladium species.<sup>18e</sup> Use of the stronger bases LiHMDS and Barton’s base (2-tert-butyl-1,1,3,3- tetramethylguanidine) shut down the reaction (entries 15 and 16) probably because of the dificulty of generating the necessary Pd−H species.<sup>20</sup>

Having determined the optimal reaction conditions, we turned our attention to an investigation of the substrate scope. Reactions of 1a with benzoxyallenes bearing an electrondonating or electron-withdrawing group at the para position of the benzene ring gave the desired products (3b−3k) in 60−96% yields with excellent enantioselectivities (>90% ee) (Table 2). Specifically, halogen atoms (3c−3e) and methoxy (3h), methylthio (3i), trifluoromethyl (3j), and trifluoromethoxy (3k) groups were compatible with the reaction conditions. In addition, ortho-fluoro- and meta-chlorosubstituted benzoxyallenes gave 3l and 3m, respectively, with high enantioselectivities. Replacing the benzene ring with a naphthalene ring (3n) or a heterocyclic ring (3o and 3p) did not afect the enantioselectivity, but an allene derived from N-Boc-piperidine gave 3q with only 76% ee. An alkoxyallene with a relatively long alkyl chain gave 3r in 84% yield with 85% ee. To our delight, alkoxyallenes derived from (S)- citronellol (3s), geraniol (3t), (S)-perillyl alcohol (3u), gemfibrozil (3v), and naproxen (3w) all gave the corresponding products in high yields with high diastereo- or enantioselectivities. Moreover, a gram-scale reaction of 1a with 2h gave 3h in 87% yield and 92% ee (see the Supporting Information). Unfortunately, when phenylallene, benzylallene, or 1-benzyloxy-1-methylallene was reacted with 1a under the standard reaction conditions, no product formation was observed (see the Supporting Information for details on limitations). Next, we investigated the scope with respect to the enone by using benzyloxyallene (2a) as the coupling partner (Table 3). Aryl ketones with various substituents on the benzene ring attached to the carbonyl group were tolerated, giving corresponding products 4b−4m. Functionality such as halogen atoms (4d−4f, 4m) and ester (4h), vinyl (4i), and alkynyl (4j) groups were retained. Moreover, the benzene ring could be replaced by a 2-naphthyl (4n), 5-benzofuranyl (4o), 5-benzothiophenyl (4p), or 2-thienyl (4q) group. However, little of the desired product was obtained when the aryl group was replaced with an alkynyl or alkyl group under the standard conditions (data not shown). Interestingly, however, we found that these substrates could be converted if $\mathrm { B } ( \mathrm { C } _ { 6 } \mathrm { F } _ { 5 } ) .$ 3 was replaced with one of the chiral spirobicyclic bisborane catalysts developed in our group (CAT1 or CAT2).<sup>21</sup> With these catalysts, reactions of alkynyl-substituted enones gave the target products (4r−4t) with high enantioselectivities, and a cyclohexyl-substituted ketone gave 4u in 69% yield with 82% ee. The activity of CAT1 and CAT2 in these reactions is similar, with the diference lying in their enantioselectivity. A matched/mismatched efect also impacts the enantioselectivity. For example, the use of ent-CAT2 with L1 in the reaction of 1r aforded 4r in 71% yield and 73% ee. Notably, even though ketone 1u has an additional enolization site on the cyclohexyl group, no side product resulting from enolate addition at this site was detected. However, replacing the cyclohexyl group in 1u with methyl or t-butyl groups results in no reaction or the formation of only trace amounts of products.

Subsequently, we tested enones with various α and β substituents. When the α substituent was ethyl, n-butyl, benzyl, cyclopropyl, or alkynyl, corresponding products 4v−

4z, respectively, were obtained in 79−90% yields with 84− 92% ee. However, the ee dropped to 76% when the ketone had an α-hydrogen atom (4aa). Interestingly, β-disubstituted enone 1bb was also reactive, giving 4bb in 42% yield with 90% ee, despite the increased steric hindrance. When we replaced the β-methyl group with a benzyl or n-butyl group, products 4cc and 4dd, which have adjacent stereogenic centers, were formed in good yields but with poor diastereoselectivity. Notably, no byproducts generated by reactions at the α position of the enones were detected in any of the above-mentioned reactions in Tables 2 and 3, perhaps because of the steric bulk of the borane catalyst. The absolute configuration of 4h was determined by X-ray single-crystal analysis (CCDC 2401879), whereas the configurations of the other products in Tables 2 and 3 were tentatively assigned by analogy.

Inspired by the fact that the combination of a transitionmetal catalyst with an organocatalyst or a Lewis acid catalyst has been successfully used for many stereodivergent reactions,<sup>22</sup> we wondered whether the catalytic system described herein could also be used for stereodivergent synthesis. Satisfyingly, we found that by varying the stereochemistry of chiral spirobicyclic bisborane catalyst CAT2, we were able to control the stereochemistry of the γ stereogenic center of the product (4ee) generated by reaction of alkynylsubstituted enone 1ee with 2a and that by varying the ligand for the palladium catalyst, we could control the stereochemistry of the δ stereogenic center (Scheme 2).<sup>23−25</sup> In this way, we were able to achieve diastereoselective formation of all four stereoisomers with high enantioselectivities.

In summary, we have developed a method for regio- and enantioselective coupling of enones with alkoxyallenes with synergistic borane/palladium catalysis. The strong inductive efect of coordination of the borane catalyst to the carbonyl group of the enone enabled trialkylamine-promoted enolization, a process that is otherwise impossible because of the high pK values of these compounds. The resulting protonated trialkylamine could protonate the palladium catalyst to initiate the palladium catalytic cycle. In this way, the pK limit for carbonyl pronucleophiles in palladium-catalyzed coupling reactions with unsaturated hydrocarbons has been increased to approximately 26. Furthermore, by combining chiral spirobicyclic bisborane catalysts with chiral palladium catalysts, we accomplished stereodivergent synthesis. We believe that such synergistic borane/transition-metal catalytic systems could push the boundaries of well-established catalytic systems with transition metals, thereby leading to the discovery of new reactions.
