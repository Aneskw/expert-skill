# Dearomative (3 + 2) Cycloadditions of 3‑Nitroheteroarenes with Allenyl Sulfones Mediated by Ion Pair Organocatalysis

ABSTRACT: We report the first example of dearomative (3 + 2) cycloadditions of 3-nitro(aza)-indole, -benzofuran, and -benzothiophene derivatives in the presence of allenyl sulfones, using sulfinate ammonium ion pairs as organocatalytic promoters. The methodology provides a new, facile, and eficient protocol for the synthesis of functionalized 2,3-fused cyclopentannulated indolines and dihydrobenzofurans.

D <sup>earomatization</sup> <sup>reactions</sup> <sup>are</sup> <sup>valuable</sup> <sup>tools</sup> <sup>in</sup> <sup>organic</sup> synthesis, generating three-dimensional (3D) molecules from easily accessible raw substrates.<sup>1</sup> However, the disruption of the stable aromatic π system makes their development challenging from both kinetic and thermodynamic viewpoints. In this context, electron-rich indoles have been used in many alkaloid syntheses, taking advantage of their C3 nucleophilicity.<sup>2</sup> In a complementary way, N-protected 3-nitroindoles have recently become privileged scafolds, with their C2�C3 double bond displaying an electrophilic behavior akin to nitrostyrenes.<sup>3</sup> In the presence of electron-rich 1,3-dipoles, several dearomatizing (3 + 2) cycloadditions have been reported with 3-nitroindoles, allowing elegant generation of tricyclic indolines. 3a,4

The addition of catalytic phosphines to allenoates generates 1,3-dipoles, which can react with Michael acceptors to form α- and/or γ-regioisomeric cyclopentenes (Scheme 1A, top).<sup>5</sup> Recently, it has been shown that nitroheteroaromatics also react with these dipoles to generate cyclopentaindolines, with an exclusive α-regioselectivity (Scheme 1A, bottom).<sup>6</sup>

As alternative electron-deficient allenes, allenyl sulfones exhibit distinct reactivity in (3 + 2) annulations in the presence of a sulfinate promoter (Scheme 1B).<sup>7,8</sup> This sulfinate is incorporated into the cycloadduct, while a sulfinate anion derived from the starting allenyl sulfone is released. As a result, sulfinate can be used in catalytic amounts if it is similar to the sulfonyl group on allene. These (3 + 2) annulations have been extended to a few classical (hetero)dipolarophiles, including acrylonitrile, methyl ketones, cyclic carbonyl compounds, or imines.<sup>9</sup> Noteworthy, employing a sulfinate−ammonium (ArSO <sup>−</sup> R N<sup>+</sup>) cooperative ion pair as a promoter has proven beneficial, expanding the scope of this chemistry to a broader range of substrates.<sup>9e−h</sup>

We were

pleased to observe the formation of compound 3a at $45 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ resulting from the cycloaddition of the transient allyl sulfone anion at the ${ \mathrm { C } } 2 { = } { \dot { \mathrm { C } } } 3$ double bond of indole. Noteworthy, compound 3a was stable under the reaction conditions, and no bis-adduct resulting from a subsequent addition to the newly formed electron-poor olefin could be detected. Optimization of the reaction conditions showed that N-Boc protection of indole was preferable (entry 2). Complete conversion of the starting material was observed, resulting in $\alpha \cdot$ and $\gamma \mathrm { - }$ regioisomers $[ \sim 9 { : } 1$ regioisomeric ratio (rr)], with compound 3b isolated in a 63% yield. Decreasing the temperature to room temperature led to a similar conversion but slightly higher yield without impact on the rr (entry 3). At a lower temperature, the reaction was less eficient (entry 4). Replacing the chloride counterion with bromine resulted in a lower conversion and yield (entry 5), while diminishing the catalyst loading was detrimental (entry 6). Notably, the nature of the quaternary ammonium salt can be easily changed, as the use of BnMe NCl in $\mathrm { C H } _ { 3 } \mathrm { C N }$ for 12 h and nBu NCl in tetrahydrofuran (THF) for 16 h performed equally well (entry 7). Finally, the reaction required both the presence of sulfinate and ammonium catalysts (entries 8 and 9) (see the Supporting Information for additional details).

Allenyl sulfone 2b (R = H) was then tested in the cycloaddition process. A higher tendency to self-condensation was previously observed with this compound.<sup>9b</sup> Gratifyingly, under the optimized conditions, the reactivity of compound 2b was selectively directed toward nitroheteroarenes, which were converted into cycloadducts 3i−3n in good yields (63−87%) and exquisite α-regioselectivity in all cases. While inert toward α-methyl allenyl sulfone 2a, 3-nitrobenzothiophene reacted with compound 2b to give compound 3o in 20% yield (50% conversion).

α-Ethylallenyl sulfone 2c proved to be significantly less reactive than compounds 2a and $^ { 2 \mathbf { b } , }$ showing no reaction with 3-nitroindole 1b. In contrast, more electrophilic 7-aza-3- nitroindole 1c was dearomatized at $0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ in the presence of this more sterically hindered sulfone.<sup>11</sup> Strikingly, in this case, azaindoline 4, isolated in 62% yield, displayed complete $\gamma \mathrm { - }$ regioselectivity, probably for steric reasons.

To have insight into the reaction mechanism, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were undertaken (see the Supporting Information for details). A model 3-nitroindole derivative, A, bearing a formyl group on the nitrogen center of the heterocycle, methanesulfonyl sulfone B, and a methane sulfinate anion associated with a tetramethylammonium cation were considered (Scheme 3).

While the formation of allyl sulfone anion C was more energetic than anticipated, the addition to indole appeared to be less energydemanding than expected for a dearomatizing process, suggesting that it is a relatively facile step. Within the cycloaddition process, the first addition step $\bar { ( } \mathbf { A } + \mathbf { C }  \mathbf { D } )$ was the determining step, even if the cyclization step (D to E) exhibited a relatively high barrier $( \Delta G ^ { \ddagger } = 1 3 . 0 \mathrm { k c a l \ m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ , with respect to separated substrates, and 15.3 kcal mol<sup>−1</sup>, with respect to the adduct D), which was unexpected for an intramolecular cyclization. This is, however, in line with previous data computed with the related dearomatizing allenoate/phosphine $\left( 3 + 2 \right)$ annulation process.<sup>6f</sup> The final step of the sequence appeared to be favorable from both kinetic and thermodynamic points of view. The stability of cycloadduct F probably drives the reaction to completion by shifting all possible equilibria and explains the whole transformation.

Considering the high energy barrier of the first step of the sequence, we then examined diferent nucleophilic promoters for the reaction. In this regard, sodium nitrites, cyanides, phosphines, or N-heterocyclic carbenes (NHCs) have already been employed to activate allenyl sulfones.<sup>9</sup> However, regardless of the promoter, only the formation of sulfonyl cycloadducts was observed, indicating that the acting catalyst in the process is the sulfinate anion itself, generated in situ.

In conclusion, we have developed the first dearomatization of 3-nitroheteroarenes with allenyl sulfones to generate $^ { 2 , 3 \cdot }$ fused cyclopentannulated heterocycles bearing a versatile vinylsulfone motif. The process is catalyzed by sulfinate anions, whether added or in situ generated, associated with a quaternary ammonium salt. Unsubstituted or α-methyl allenyl sulfones led to the exclusive or favored formation of the α- regioisomeric cycloadducts, while the less reactive more hindered α-ethyl allenyl sulfone furnished exclusively the γ- regioisomer in reaction with 7-aza-3-nitroindole. The mechanism, supported by a DFT study, suggests a highly energetic sulfonyl anion formation, followed by a two-step cycloaddition and a barrierless sulfinate release. The reactivity of azanitroindoles is especially interesting to exploit, revealing 3D molecules that are pharmaceutically relevant. The diferent derivatizations conducted further demonstrate the versatility of these cycloadducts. We are currently looking to extend the methodology to enantioselective processes through use of chiral ammonium salts.
