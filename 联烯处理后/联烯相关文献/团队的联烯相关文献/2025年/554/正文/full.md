ABSTRACT: Herein, a rhodium-catalyzed highly chemo-, regio-, and stereo-selective linear cross coupling reaction of terminal alkynes with readily available 2,3-allenols aforded the synthetically and biologically versatile 3-alkyn-2(E)-enones/enals. The reaction exhibits a perfect match of the reactivities for these two classes of compounds since the well-known self-reacting products of both reactants were not formed. The reaction enjoys a wide substrate scope under ambient conditions. Furthermore, the synthetic potentials have been demonstrated by utilizing this reaction as a pivotal step: a very concise two-step total synthesis of naturally occurring isotaxifolial D has been achieved; an eficient synthesis of dehydroacitretin by applying this reaction two times has also been executed. Through mechanistic studies including Dlabeling experiments, it is proposed that the reaction proceeded via

The reaction circumvents the use of a stoichiometric amount of base under the catalysis of a single Rh catalyst since terminal alkynylmetalation is base free, and the catalyst is regenerated by aerobic oxidation in the presence of proton.

## ■ INTRODUCTION

Transition metal-catalyzed reactions have constantly been providing highly eficient methodologies for the eficient formation of covalent bonds. With the fine-tuning of the ligand structure and metal, the regio-, chemo-, and stereoselectivity may be precisely controlled. Terminal alkynes make up an important class of compounds for such reactions in organic synthesis. For example, the well-known Sonogashira coupling of terminal alkynes with 1-alkenyl halides utilizes the highly reactive C−X bond to form the sp $\mathrm { \dot { C } } { \cdot } { \mathsf { s p } } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { C }$ bonds under the co-catalysis of Pd and $\operatorname { C u } ( \operatorname { I } ) .$ 1 On the other hand, allenes have also been becoming one of the most important classes of organic compounds.<sup>2</sup> Nowadays, the allenation of terminal alkynes (ATA reaction) has made mono-, di-, and trisubstituted allenes readily available.<sup>2d</sup> The reactions of terminal alkynes with allenes have merely been sporadically studied. Of our interest is the formation of 1-alkynyl π-allylic metallic species A, which may be converted to enynes A′ and/ or the regioisomeric enynes $\dot { \mathbf { A } } ^ { \prime \prime }$ upon further trapping, although the formations of non-conjugated alken-4-yn-2-yl metallic species B and C were also observed (Figure 1a).<sup>3</sup> Beyond terminal alkynes, decarboxylative alkynylation of allenes with alkynyl carboxylic acids<sup>4</sup> and transition metal-catalyzed reactions of haloalkynes with allenes<sup>5</sup> had also been developed. In 2005, Alcaide and Almendros reported the reaction of terminal alkynes with 2,3-allenols formed π-allylic metallic species A1, which readily underwent cyclic etherification to aford D-type 3-alkynyl-2,5-dihydrofurnans due to the presence of the nucleophilic OH group (Figure 1b).<sup>6</sup> We report herein the observation of the Rh-catalyzed highly regio- and stereoselective aerobic cross-coupling of terminal alkynes with 2,3- allenols afording the stereo-defined 1,3-enynals or enyl ketones with an exclusive E selectivity (Figure 1c), which are a type of common unit in natural or bioactive products (Figure 1d).<sup>7</sup>

## ■ RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

As shown in Figure 3, terminal alkynols with diferent lengths of carbon chain between the C−C triple bond and the hydroxy group aforded the corresponding products (E)-3aa∼(E)-3ca in decent yields, and the reaction is robust since no protection is required for the free OH group. Diferent alkyl-substituted terminal alkynes without the hydroxy group also worked to aford (E)-3da and (E)-3ea. Even the highly strained cyclopropyl group survived, afording (E)-3fa. Terminal alkynes bearing various highly synthetically versatile functional groups, such as chloro (1g), cyano (1h), and ester (1i), may also be applied, afording (E)-3ga∼(E)-3ia. Even the reaction of p-formyl phenylacetylene furnished the corresponding product (E)-3la with the formyl group untouched, whose structure was further confirmed by X-ray crystallographic study.<sup>14</sup> Diferent aryl- and heteroaryl-substituted terminal alkynes 1m∼1o also worked to aford the corresponding 3- alkyn-2(E)-enones (E)-3ma∼(E)-3oa. However, heteroarylsubstituted terminal alkyne 2-pyridinylacetylene 2p aforded trace (E)-3pa, with 88% recovery of 2a. When hepta-1,6-diyne was applied, the selectivity is remarkable: the mono-crosscoupling product (E)-3qa was obtained in 41% yield, together with the double-cross-coupling product (E)-3qaa in only 5% yield. With regard to 2,3-allenols, R<sup>5</sup> may be alkyl groups ((E)- 3db, (E)-3rb, (E)-3be) or electron-rich and electron-deficient aryl groups ((E)-3aa∼(E)-3qa, (E)-3dc, (E)-3sd, (E)-3bf, and (E)-3bg). Heteroaryl-substituted 2,3-allenol 2h also delivered the product (E)-3bh. It is worthy to mention that even the aldehydes (E)-3bi and (E)-3si∼(E)-3ui with a very high E/Z selectivity were available from the reaction of terminal alkynes 1b and 1s∼1u with 2,3-butadienol $2 \mathbf { i } . ^ { 1 5 }$ To our delight, the reaction of even di-substituted 2,3-allenols 2j and 2k with terminal alkyne 1a also aforded the desired products (E)-3aj and (E)-3ak in 39% and 34% yields, respectively. However, when the 4-position-substituted $^ { 2 , 3 \cdot }$ -allenols 2l and 2m were treated under the standard conditions with terminal alkyne 1a, the target products were aforded in low yields. Unfortunately, when tri- and tetra-substituted 2,3-allenols ${ \bf 2 n } { \bf \sim } { \bf 2 p }$ were treated with 1a under the standard conditions, (E)-3an∼(E)-3ap were not formed; the reaction of tri-substituted 2,3-allenol 2n with 1a aforded 12% of the cycloisomerization product $2 \mathbf { n } ^ { \prime \prime }$ with 81% recovery of 2n. ## ■ CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we have developed a conceptually new approach for the enyne synthesis via a single rhodium-catalyzed crosscoupling reaction of terminal alkynes with 2,3-allenols. This protocol enables an eficient and highly selective preparation of (E)-3-alkynyl enones/enals under a set of ambient conditions, which could serve as platform molecules for the synthesis of many other useful molecules due to the accommodation of a wide array of highly reactive functional groups. The synthetic potential of this methodology has been demonstrated via derivatization of bio-active molecules, the eficient total syntheses of isotaxifolial D, and the synthesis of dehydroaci tretin. A mechanism has been proposed for the observed highly selective formation of (E)-3-alkynyl enones/enals. The use of a base is not required since the terminal alkynylmetalation proceeds readily under base-free conditions, and the catalytically active rhodium catalyst has been regenerated via aerobic oxidation with the help of HOAc. The development of many other types of such reactions with both types of readily available terminal alkynes and allenes as the reactants for enyne syntheses is highly expected and is being actively pursued in our laboratory.
