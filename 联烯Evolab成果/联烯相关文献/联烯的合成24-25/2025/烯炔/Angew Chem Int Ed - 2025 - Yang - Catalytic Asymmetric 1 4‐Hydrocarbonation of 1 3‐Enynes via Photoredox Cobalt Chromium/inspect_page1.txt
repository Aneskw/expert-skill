      "text": "Synthetic Methods\nCatalytic Asymmetric 1,4-Hydrocarbonation of 1,3-Enynes via\nPhotoredox/Cobalt/Chromium Triple Catalysis\nJu-Song Yang, Xing-Yu Wang, Yong-Yao Li, Fu-Min Zhang,* Xiao-Ming Zhang, and\nYong-Qiang Tu*\nAbstract: A synergistic photoredox/cobalt/chromium\ntriple catalysis system for regioselective, enantioselec-\ntive, and diastereoselective 1,4-hydrocarbonation of\nreadily available 1,3-enyne precursors was explored,\nproviding a modular synthetic platform for various\ntrisubstituted axially chiral allenes bearing an extra\ncentral chirality. The protocol features a broad substrate\nscope, good functional group tolerance, excellent selec-\ntivity, and mild reaction conditions. Furthermore, a\npossible reaction mechanism is proposed based on\nnumerous control experiments and density functional\ntheory calculations.\nIntroduction\nProchiral 1,3-enynes have received extensive attention in\nvarious asymmetric synthetic transformations using 1,4-\ndifunctionalization procedures because of their multiple\nreaction sites and easily availability.\n[1–3]\nIn particular, the\ncatalytic asymmetric 1,4-hydrofunctionalization of 1,3-\nenynes has emerged as a method for accurately adding a\nhydrogen atom and carbon or heteroatom moiety in sta-\ntionary conjugate π systems.\n[4–9,11]\nAccordingly, various\ntransition metal (TM) catalysts (palladium,\n[4]\nrhodium,\n[5]\ncopper,\n[6]\nscandium,\n[7]\netc.) and organocatalysts\n[9]\nhave been\ndeveloped to synthesize silyl-, boryl-, and aryl-substituted\naxially chiral allenes via an ionic pathway (Scheme 1A, top).\nHowever, in sharp contrast to these ionic pathways, the\nradical 1,4-hydrofunctionalization of 1,3-enynes has yet to\nbe exploited owing to the canonical migratory insertion of\nmetal hydride into olefin units typically generating more\nstable alkyl metal intermediates rather than dissociative\npropargyl or short-lived allenic radicals.\n[10]\nTo the best of\nour knowledge, only one example has been reported; very\nrecently, Fu et al. achieved chiral nickel hydride–catalyzed\nasymmetric coupling of prochiral 1,3-enynes with racemic\nsecondary alkyl electrophiles to simultaneously produce\ncentral and axial chirality (Scheme 1B, bottom).\n[11]\nThere-\nfore, exploring new radical formation modes from 1,3-\nenynes to achieve mechanistic innovations and obtain useful\nmultiple chiral allene architectures is urgent.\nAs the radical equivalent of Brønsted acid proton\ntransfer, TM-catalyzed (cobalt (Co), iron (Fe), manganese\n(Mn), etc.) metal hydride hydrogen atom transfer (MHAT)\nreactions of alkenes represent a powerful and practical\nmethod for constructing diverse C \u0000 C and C-heteroatom\nbonds.\n[12–18]\nAmong them, tandem MHAT/1,2-addition was\ndeveloped by Shenvi et al. (2018)\n[14]\nand Shi et al. (2021)\n[15]\nto couple alkyl-substituted olefins, allenes, or 1,3-dienes\nwith radicalophilic imines or aldehydes, respectively. For the\ncatalytic asymmetric version, a recent breakthrough was\nelegantly disclosed by Wang et al., employing modified\nphotoredox/Co/chromium (Cr) triple–catalytic systems for\nenantioselective 1,2-addition of benzoyloxy-substituted 1,3-\ndienes to synthesize aldehydes bearing an indispensable\nbenzoyloxy protecting group, in which the oxygen atom in\nthe substrates plays an essential coordination role\n(Scheme 1B).\n[16]\nRetrospectively, MHAT-meditated regio-\nand chemoselective 1,4-hydrofunctionalization of more\nstructurally complex 1,3-enyens substrates was rarely studied\nexcept for a remarkable racemic version of research work by\nCarreira’s group in 2006;\n[17]\nunfortunately, only azodicarbox-\nylate was used as an electrophile, which led to the\ncorresponding propargyl derivatives. To fill this gap, partic-\nularly the catalytic asymmetric version, we speculated that if\nother electrophiles, such as aldehydes or imines, were\nreacted with the chiral propargyl radical species derived\nfrom prochiral 1,3-enynes, the unconventional asymmetric\n1,4-hydrofunctionalization of 1,3-enynes would occur; fur-\nthermore, the novel synthetic method would provide an\nefficient approach toward α-allenol, a unique structural\nmotif bearing both central and axial chirality, widely found\nin natural products, pharmaceuticals, functional materials,\nchiral ligands, and key synthetic intermediates (Fig-\nure 1).\n[19–20]\nHowever, this attractive strategy has several\nsynthetic challenges that must be addressed. 1) Unlike the\n1,2-addition of an allyl group via a suitable six-membered\ntransition state, the 1,2-addition of propargyl-containing\nintermediates to aldehydes via an extremely distorted cyclic\ntransition state leads to unmanageable regioselectivity,\n[*] J.-S.
