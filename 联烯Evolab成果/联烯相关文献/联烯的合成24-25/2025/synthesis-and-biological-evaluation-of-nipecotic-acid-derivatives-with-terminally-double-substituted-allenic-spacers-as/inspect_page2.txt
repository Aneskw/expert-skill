Upon inhibition of mGAT1\npredominantly located on neuronal cells the reuptake of GABA\nis thought to be shifted toward mGAT4. Since mGAT4 is mainly\npresent on glial cells, the neurotransmitter is largely directed\ntoward the latter. In the glial cells GABA undergoes a metabolic\ndegradation, whereas in the neurons it is reused for synaptic\nrelease. Upon mGAT1 inhibition the latter pathway of GABA,\nits transport in neurons, is partially or even fully reduced. This is\nthought to diminish the amount of neurotransmitter for GABA\nsignaling and, thus to increase the susceptibility to epileptic\nseizures.\n6\nAs mentioned above, inhibition of mGAT1 is thought to\nincrease susceptibility to epileptic seizures, as it reduces the\namount of GABA taken up by mGAT1 into neurons and\nsupplied to the neurotransmitter pool.
