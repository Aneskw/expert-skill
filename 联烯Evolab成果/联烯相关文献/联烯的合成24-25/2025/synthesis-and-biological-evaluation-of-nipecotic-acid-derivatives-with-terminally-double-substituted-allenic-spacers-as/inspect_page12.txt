Motor impairments, defined as the inability\nto remain on the rotating rod for 1 min, were noted in mice that\nreceived DDPM-3960 at the dose of 60 mg/kg (p < 0.01 vs\nvehicle-treated mice).\nGrip Strength Test. The test compound DDPM-3960 used at\ndoses of 10, 30, 60 mg/kg did not affect animals’ muscular\nstrength measured in the grip strength test (F[3,20] = 1.522, p >\n0.05).\nTo sum up, motor coordination deficits are one of the most\nfrequent adverse effects of AEDs, and this effect might be a\nserious limitation of antiepileptic pharmacotherapy. We\ndemonstrated that the test compound DDPM-3960 induced\nsome motor deficits in the rotarod test only at the highest dose\ntested, but it did not negatively influence muscular strength of\nexperimental animals.\nEffect on Locomotor Activity. The locomotor activity test\nwas used to assess the effect of DDPM-3960 on animals’ general\nactivity. Drug-induced decrease of locomotor activity (i.e.,\nsedation) or hyperlocomotion might give false positive results in\nsome in vivo tests. To exclude this obstacle, we measured\nlocomotor activity of mice at selected time points at which\nfurther assays were conducted: 1 min (important for the\nassessment of the results obtained in the four-plate and hot plate\ntests), 5 min (important for the assessment of the results\nobtained in the elevated plus maze test, capsaicin test and the\nfirst phase of the formalin test), 6 min (important for the\nassessment of the results obtained in the forced swim test) and\n30 min (important for the assessment of the results obtained in\nthe second phase of the formalin test).\nLocomotor activity was significantly affected by treatment\n(F[3,112] = 128.1, p < 0.0001). Time also affected the results\nsignificantly (F[3,112] = 159.5, p < 0.0001) and drug × time\ninteraction was also significant (F[9,112] = 49.67, p < 0001).\nPost hoc analysis revealed that in the first min of the test, DDPM-\n3960 did not affect animals’ locomotor activity. In the fifth and\nsixth mins of the locomotor activity test the doses 30 and 60 mg/\nkg significantly (p < 0.05 and p < 0.01 vs control mice, for both\ntime points, respectively) reduced locomotor activity of mice. In\nthe 30th min of the test, all doses of DDPM-3960 significantly (p\n< 0.0001) affected the locomotor activity of mice. At this time\npoint, the dose 2.5 mg/kg increased, while doses 30 and 60 mg/\nkg decreased the number of light-beam crossings (Figure 6).\nAnxiolytic-like Activity. Four-Plate Test.
