However, compared to alkyne derivative-based approaches, the asymmetric functionalization of allenic C(sp\n2\n)−H\nbonds remains a challenge primarily due to the intertwined demands of chemo-, regio-, and enantioselectivity. Here, we report a\ncopper-catalyzed asymmetric cyanation of allenic C(sp\n2\n)−H bonds by using a substituted oxazoline chiral ligand. This strategy\nefficiently transforms low-substituted racemic allenes into highly functionalized, axially chiral allenyl nitriles in a highly chemo-,\nregio-, and enantioselective manner.\nKEYWORDS: enantioselective cyanation, C−H functionalization, radical, chiral allene, copper catalysis\n■\nINTRODUCTION\nAxially chiral allenes are a class of significant organic molecules,\ncharacterized by their distinctive orthogonal twisted π-\nsystems.\n1\nFunctionalized allenes often exhibit notable physio-\nlogical activities, such as enzyme inhibition, cytotoxicity, and\nantibacterial properties (Scheme 1a).\n2\nAs a result, structural\nmodifications of allene compounds or their incorporation into\npharmaceutical molecules to enhance biological activity\nrepresent a key direction for future development in this field.\n3\nOver the past decade, significant progress has been made in\nthe development of radical synthesis methods for allenes.\n4\nAmong these advancements, the asymmetric transformation of\nallenyl radicals has emerged as a powerful strategy for\nconstructing axially chiral allenes (Scheme 1b, left). Our\ngroup previously reported the first copper-catalyzed radical 1,4-\nbifunctionalization of 1,3-enynes, achieving asymmetric 1,4-\noxycyanation and offering a novel method for synthesizing\nelusive axially chiral allenes.\n5\nSubsequently, Deng et al.\ndemonstrated a stereoselective decarboxylation strategy for\n1,3-enynes using a photoinduced dual catalytic system.\n6\nMeanwhile, Liu\n7\nand Guo\n8\nindependently achieved copper-\ncatalyzed asymmetric 1,4-alkylalkynylation and arylalkynylation\nvia allenyl radical-terminal alkyne coupling.
