CONCLUSIONS AND PERSPECTIVES\nIn this Account, we have summarized our recent studies on the\nallenation of terminal alkynes (ATA) with paraformaldehyde,\naromatic and aliphatic aldehydes, and ketones in the presence\nof a proper amine to a ﬀord monosubstituted allenes, 1,3-\ndisubstitued allenes, and trisubstituted allenes, respectively. On\nthe other hand, we have also demonstrated chiral ligand\nstrategy and chiral amine strategy for the enantioselective\nallenation of terminal alkynes (EATA) with aldehydes to\nproduce optically active 1,3-disubstituted allenes. Notably, the\nEATA reactions have been applied in the e ﬃcient and highly\nenantioselective syntheses of some natural 1,3-disubstituted\nallenes including laballenic acid, insect pheromone, methyl\n(R)-8-hydroxyocta-5,6-dienoate, phlomic acid, and lamenal-\nlenic acid, as well as some non-allene natural γ-butyrolactones\nincluding xestospongienes (E, F, G, and H), ( R)-4-\ntetradecalactone, (S)-4-tetradecalactone, (R)-γ-palmitolactone,\nand ( R)-4-decalactone. Owing to the readily available starting\nmaterials and easy operations, the ATA reactions will stimulate\nthe research on allene chemistry with no doubt. The\nachievements of EATA reactions are promising and provide\nnew opportunities in constructing central chiralities via axis-to-\ncenter approach.\nOf course, there are still some unconquered challenges: (1)\nSince the reactions are usually performed above re ﬂux\nconditions, it would be better if the reaction may be conducted\nunder milder conditions for some sensitive functionalities with\nbetter catalytic recipes. (2) The EATA reaction via the chiral\nligand strategy is quite underdeveloped. The scope of terminal\nalkynes is limited to tertiary and secondary α-alkynols. New\nligands should be designed to upgrade this technology. (3)\nThe EATA reaction with ketones has not been realized.
