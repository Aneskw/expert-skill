      "text": "German Edition: DOI: 10.1002/ange.201914747Polymers\nInternational Edition: DOI: 10.1002/anie.201914747\nAccess to Hydroxy-Functionalized Polypropylene through\nCoordination Polymerization\nYuanhao Zhong, Iskander Douair, Tiantian Wang, Chunji Wu,* Laurent Maron,* and\nDongmei Cui*\nAbstract: Preparation of polyethylenes containing hydroxy\ngroups has been already industrialized through radical copoly-\nmerization under harsh conditions followed by alcoholysis. By\ncontrast, hydroxy-functionalized polypropylene has proven\na rather challenging goal in polymer science. Propylene can/C29t\nbe polymerized through a radical mechanism, and its coordi-\nnation copolymerization with polar monomers is frustrated by\ncatalyst poisoning. Herein, we report a new strategy to reach\nthis target. The coordination polymerization of allenes by rare-\nearth-metal precursors affords pure 1,2-regulated polyallenes,\nwhich are facilely transformed into poly(allyl alcohol) ana-\nlogues by subsequent hydroboration/oxidation. Strikingly, the\ncopolymerization of allenes and propylene gives unprece-\ndented hydroxy-functionalized polypropylene after post-poly-\nmerization modification. Mechanistic elucidation by DFT\nsimulation suggests kinetic rather than thermodynamic control.\nThe synthesis of hydroxy-functionalized (co)polymers, in\nparticular polyolefins, has been a major focus of polymer\nscientists because the hydroxy group endows the materials\nwith hydrophilicity and excellent surface properties, and\nfurthermore, it is a reactive group that enables access to other\nfunctionalities. For instance, polyethylene (PE) is the most\nfamous and widely applied hydrophobic plastic,\n[1] whilst its\nhydroxy-functionalized derivatives such as poly(vinyl alco-\nhol) (PV A) and poly(ethylene-vinyl alcohol) (EVOH) pos-\nsess rather unique properties and applications in different\nfields.\n[2] To date the copolymerization of ethylene with\na broad scope of functional vinyl monomers has been\nthoroughly investigated and achieved great successes,\n[3]\nexcept for hydroxy functionalization. Fortunately, radical\npolymerization of vinyl acetate or copolymerization with\nethylene under high temperature and pressure followed by\npost-polymerization alcoholysis affords PV A and EVOH,\nwhich is a rather efficient industrialized method.\n[4] By\ncontrast, synthesis of the corresponding hydroxy-functional-\nized polypropylene (PP) from the direct polymerization of\nallylic alcohol (AA) or its copolymerization with propylene\nposes serious problems. Although allyl alcohol (AA) is\na stable chemical, it is hard to propagate into a high molecular\nweight P AA through radical polymerization even with the\nassistance of a Lewis acid;\n[5] furthermore anionic and\ncoordination catalysts react with the hydroxy group rather\nthan the C =C bond. A more efficient manner of obtaining\na linear P AA with high molecular weight (ca. 30 kDa) and\na block copolymer with styrene P(AA-St) was reported\nrecently by Hillmyer and co-workers through reversible\naddition–fragmentation chain transfer (RAFT) radical poly-\nmerization of the Boc-protected (bis(tert-butyloxy carbon-\nate)) vinyl ester or vinyl amide followed by post-polymeri-\nzation reduction.\n[6] The copolymerization of propene with\nallyl acetate is another way to reach PP that includes hydroxy\nfunctionalities after hydrolysis, but the insertion rate of allyl\nacetate was poor.\n[7] Therefore, the coordination polymeri-\nzation approach used to fabricate PP hasn/C29t been applied to\nprepare P AA or hydroxy-containing PP , since even the Boc-\nprotected polar monomers are poisonous to the employed\ncoordination catalysts.\nAllene compounds are widely employed as building\nblocks in the field of organic synthesis due to their highly\nactive cumulative double bonds.\n[8] They have also attracted\nthe attention of polymer scientists because the corresponding\npolymers possess double bonds that can be transformed into\nother functionalities, for example, through hydrogenation,\n[9]\nepoxidation,[10] and hydrosilylation,[11] in post-polymerization\nmodification steps. However, the high activity of the cumu-\nlative double bonds causes difficulty in controlling the\npolymer microstructures through either radical or cationic\npolymerization.\n[12] Well-controlled polymerization of allenes\nis achieved by using coordination catalysts, of which the Ti-\nbased systems [10] , h3-allyl NiOCOCF 3,[13] and the rare-earth-\nmetal catalysts[14] display almost perfect 2,3-selectivity. Never-\ntheless, the resultant polymers contain internal C =C double\nbonds that are chemically stable and resistant to further\ntransformation.
