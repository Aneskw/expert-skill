AgOTf was used to remove\nchloride ions in the heterogeneous catalyst Pd-AmP-CNC,\nthereby generating highly active Pd\nII, which results in high\nefficiency of the heterogeneous catalytic system.\nPalladium-catalyzed oxidative carbonylations have been\nwidely used for the introduction of carbonyl groups into\norganic molecules, which provides a basis for the streamlined\nconstruction of valuable products from various feedstocks.\n[1]\nAs a powerful approach towards direct oxidative carbon-\nylations of aliphatic hydrocarbons, these reactions are attrac-\ntive for applications in industry. A central challenge in\npalladium-catalyzed oxidative carbonylation reactions is\nachieving high palladium efficiency, and at the same time,\nrealizing well-controlled selectivity and recovery of the metal\nin the transformations.\n[2]\nOver the past six years, our group has developed efficient\noxidative carbonylation reactions of enallenes catalyzed by\nPd(OAc)\n2 or Pd(TFA) 2.[1j, 3] As shown in Scheme 1 a, the\nolefin group of enallene A coordinates to Pd II (Int-A),\ntriggering allenic C(sp 3)/C0H cleavage to give Int-B. After\na carbon monoxide insertion and a subsequent olefin\ninsertion, this reaction allows the construction of a cyclo-\npentenone intermediate ( Int-C), which can be transformed\ninto different useful products. In these transformations,\ncoordination of the olefin to Pd\nII (Int-A) is essential for the\nallenic C(sp3)/C0H cleavage to afford Int-B. Replacement of the\nolefin group by an alkyl or aryl group completely shuts down\nthe reactivity of the allene.\n[4] Inspired by these previous\nstudies, we were interested in investigating the oxidative\ncarbonylation of allenes bearing a nitrogen-containing func-\ntionality (NHR, Scheme 1 b).
